# AI Writing/Illustration: 130-1500x Less CO2 Than Humans (Per Task)

**Topic:** GHG Emissions - Numbers Compilation
**Source:** Tomlinson, Black, Patterson & Torrance, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-54271-x

## Key Numbers

| Metric | Value | Context |
|--------|-------|---------|
| AI text generation | 130-1500x less CO2e | Per page compared to human writers |
| AI illustration | 310-2900x less CO2e | Per image compared to human illustrators |
| Systems studied | ChatGPT, BLOOM, DALL-E2, Midjourney | Generative AI systems |

## Methodology Notes

- Comparative analysis between AI systems and human individuals performing equivalent tasks
- Human emissions include: transportation, food, heating/cooling workspace, equipment
- AI emissions include: data center energy consumption, hardware manufacturing (amortized)
- Per-page and per-image metrics enable direct comparison
- Published in Nature Scientific Reports - peer-reviewed methodology

## Critical Caveats (from the paper itself)

- **Does NOT account for social impacts**: professional displacement, legal issues
- **Does NOT account for rebound effects**: AI may increase total content production
- **AI is not a substitute for all human tasks**: comparison limited to specific use cases
- The comparison assumes AI output is equivalent in quality/utility to human output
- Lower per-task emissions could lead to MORE total emissions if usage scales dramatically

## Why This Matters

- First peer-reviewed comparison of AI vs human emissions for equivalent tasks
- Provides concrete numbers (130-2900x range) rather than vague claims
- The explicit caveats about rebound effects are critical for honest assessment
- Shows AI CAN reduce per-task emissions while acknowledging systemic concerns
- 43 citations - influential for "AI-for-climate" narrative discussions
