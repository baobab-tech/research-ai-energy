# Microsoft FY2025 emissions rose 26% to 21.1 MtCO2e; Scope 2 market-based jumped 10x after it stopped buying non-additional RECs

**Topic:** GHG emissions — corporate primary disclosure; effect of tightening renewable-energy accounting
**Source:** Microsoft Corporation, *2026 Environmental Sustainability Report* and Environmental Data Fact Sheet (FY2025 data), 2026
**Type:** corporate disclosure
**URL:** https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/2026-Microsoft-Environmental-Data-Fact-Sheet-PDF.pdf
**Published:** 2026-07

## Finding

Microsoft's total FY2025 emissions were 21,121,000 mtCO2e on a market-based basis, up 26.5% from 16,693,000 in FY2024. Two distinct things drove it. Scope 3 rose 12% to 18.24 Mt on data-centre construction. Scope 2 market-based rose 10.4x, from 259,090 to 2,707,428 mtCO2e — almost entirely a methodology change, because Microsoft stopped crediting itself with unbundled renewable energy certificates it judged "non-additional." The FY2024 figure of 259,090 t was therefore an artefact of REC purchasing, not of clean supply: location-based Scope 2 in the same year was 9,955,368 t, 38x higher.

## Methodology

Self-reported under the GHG Protocol, with a parallel "management's criteria" table (Table 1B) reporting a different set of figures alongside the GHGP table (Table 1A) — two headline totals in one document. Market-based Scope 2 is the reported basis for the total. The FY2025 jump reflects Microsoft's own decision to exclude unbundled RECs that do not cause new clean generation; this makes FY2025 more honest and makes the prior-year series non-comparable.

## Limitations and conflicts

Self-reported by the emitter. The FY2024-to-FY2025 comparison mixes a real increase (construction, electricity +24%) with a methodology correction, and a reader taking the 26% headline as physical growth would be wrong. No AI-specific emissions are disclosed; Microsoft reports datacentre water and electricity by location but not by workload. The existence of two total-emissions tables on different criteria makes the report difficult to reconcile against peers. Microsoft is also the employer of the authors of 026, whose central finding is that outside per-query energy estimates are 4-20x too high.
