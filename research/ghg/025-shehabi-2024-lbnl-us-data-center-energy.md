# US data centres used 176 TWh in 2023 (4.4% of national electricity); 2028 range is 325-580 TWh (6.7-12.0%)

**Topic:** GHG emissions — US aggregate data-centre electricity, the underlying driver of US AI emissions
**Source:** Shehabi, Smith, Hubbard, Newkirk, Lei, Siddik, Holecek, Koomey, Masanet, Sartor — Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL-2001637), 2024
**Type:** agency report (US DOE national laboratory)
**URL:** https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/lbnl-2024-united-states-data-center-energy-usage-report_1.pdf
**Published:** 2024-12

## Finding

The report that ended the "data-centre energy is flat" consensus. US data-centre electricity sat near 60 TWh from 2014 to 2016, rose to 76 TWh (1.9% of US electricity) by 2018, and reached 176 TWh (4.4%) in 2023 — more than doubling between 2017 and 2023. GPU-accelerated servers for AI are named as the specific cause of the inflection. The 2028 projection is deliberately published as a range, 325-580 TWh or 6.7-12.0% of forecast US electricity, because the authors judge the AI segment too immature and too opaque to support a point estimate.

## Methodology

Bottom-up installed-base model: equipment shipments by class, multiplied by modelled operational (not nameplate) power, multiplied by modelled PUE derived from cooling-system type and facility location. Servers are classified as conventional, AI non-accelerated, and AI accelerated (two or more accelerators). The authors explicitly reject top-down load-announcement tracking because it double-counts projects that have not selected a power provider. The 2028 range is generated by varying three things: accelerator shipment volumes, how hard and how often accelerated hardware is run, and cooling-system efficiency. Pre-2024 values are historical estimates; 2024-2028 are scenarios.

## Limitations and conflicts

Funded by the US DOE Industrial Efficiency and Decarbonization Office under contract DE-AC02-05CH11231. Input data was purchased from Omdia, Dell'Oro, S&P Global and IDC — the same proprietary, unauditable vendor datasets the IEA relies on, so LBNL and IEA figures are not independent. Draft review came from approximately 50 organisations described as "major data center equipment manufacturers and data center end users/owners," meaning the industry being measured reviewed the numbers. The authors state directly that "the lack of data availability significantly limits the analysis in this report." The report gives electricity, not emissions; converting to CO2 requires a grid intensity assumption the report does not supply. The 50% utilisation assumption behind the 74-132 GW figure is a generalisation.
