# AI vs Human Carbon Emissions for Writing and Illustration

**Topic:** GHG Emissions - Training (Comparative Analysis)
**Source:** Tomlinson, Black, Patterson & Torrance, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-54271-x

## Insight

This Nature Scientific Reports paper provides comparative emissions data between AI systems (ChatGPT, BLOOM, DALL-E2, Midjourney) and human workers for equivalent writing and illustration tasks. While often cited to suggest AI is "greener," the authors explicitly note that emissions analyses do not account for social impacts like professional displacement, legality issues, or rebound effects. The study acknowledges AI cannot substitute for all human tasks.

## Key Data

- AI systems emit 130-1500x less CO2e per page of text vs human writers
- AI illustration systems emit 310-2900x less CO2e per image vs human illustrators
- Comparison is per-task, not accounting for training costs amortized
- Does NOT account for: rebound effects, professional displacement, or legality issues
- Authors note AI is "not a substitute for all human tasks"

## Notes

- Published in Nature Scientific Reports - peer-reviewed, credible journal
- Authors from UC Irvine and University of Kansas
- Important caveats often ignored when this paper is cited
- Per-query emissions are low, but massive scale changes equation
- Does not address training emissions specifically - focuses on inference
- 43 citations indicates moderate academic impact
- Methodology: Estimated from published energy consumption data
