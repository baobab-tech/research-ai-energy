# IEA 2026 update: data centres consumed 485 TWh in 2025, on track to ~950 TWh by 2030, with CO2 reaching ~350 Mt by 2035

**Topic:** GHG emissions — most recent authoritative aggregate and the first IEA CO2 figure for data centres
**Source:** International Energy Agency, *Key Questions on Energy and AI*, 2026
**Type:** agency report
**URL:** https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary
**Published:** 2026-04-16

## Finding

The IEA's one-year follow-up to *Energy and AI* holds its central trajectory almost unchanged despite a capital-expenditure surge that might have been expected to push it up. Data-centre electricity consumption was 485 TWh in 2025 and is projected to roughly double to about 950 TWh by 2030 (~3% of global electricity). The update supplies the CO2 number the 2025 report withheld: around 350 MtCO2 by 2035, about 2% of global electricity-sector emissions. Electricity consumption by AI-focused data centres specifically triples over 2025-2030, growing faster than the data-centre total.

## Methodology

Same modelling frame as the 2025 report, re-based on 2025 outturn data. The IEA states its central projection "remains close to the trajectory set out in the IEA's 2025 report." New in this edition is an explicit analysis of value-chain bottlenecks — turbines, transformers, grid interconnection, chip supply — which the IEA uses to argue *against* the more aggressive near-term scenarios while acknowledging upside after 2030. So the stability of the central case is not the absence of new information; it is capex pressure and supply constraint offsetting each other.

## Limitations and conflicts

The ~350 Mt CO2 for 2035 is a scenario output conditional on the projected 60% clean-electricity share materialising; if grids decarbonise more slowly, the figure rises roughly proportionally. The same proprietary-data dependency as the 2025 report applies. "AI-focused data centres" remains a facility classification, not a workload measurement — a facility is either counted as AI-focused or it is not. The report gives no uncertainty interval around the 485 TWh outturn figure.
