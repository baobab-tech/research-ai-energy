# LLM-Powered Chatbots Require Life-Cycle Carbon Analysis

**Topic:** GHG Emissions - Inference
**Source:** Jiang, Sonne, Li, You & You, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eng.2024.04.002

## Insight

This paper argues that attention to AI emissions must extend beyond training to encompass the entire life-cycle of LLM-powered chatbots. The authors identify eight main phases throughout the life cycle that contribute to energy consumption and carbon emissions: R&D of LLMs, hardware manufacturing (GPUs, supercomputers), training, fine-tuning, updating, data/operations management, chatbot services, and material recycling. They propose system-level solutions with three strategic pathways to mitigate these footprints.

## Key Data

- Training, fine-tuning, and updating intelligent chatbots consume substantial amounts of electricity resulting in significant carbon emissions
- Hardware manufacturing (GPUs and supercomputers) contributes carbon emissions to varying extents
- Data/operations management and material recycling supporting chatbot services are also associated with carbon emissions
- The paper identifies eight distinct life-cycle phases that need to be accounted for

## Notes

- This is a perspective/opinion piece in the journal Engineering (Elsevier)
- Strong emphasis on "reshaping the industry and its environmental implications at this early stage of development"
- Authors call for rethinking mitigation pathways of life-cycle energy usage and carbon emissions
- Multi-institution collaboration: authors from China, Denmark, and Cornell University
- 57 citations indicates significant influence in the field (as of Jan 2026)
