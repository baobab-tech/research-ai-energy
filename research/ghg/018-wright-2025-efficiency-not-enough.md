# Efficiency Is Not Enough: A Critical Perspective on Environmentally Sustainable AI

**Topic:** GHG Emissions - Systemic Analysis
**Source:** Wright, Igel & Samuel, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3724500

## Insight

This 2025 paper from Communications of the ACM argues that efficiency improvements alone are insufficient to make AI environmentally sustainable. The authors - from the University of Copenhagen and King's College London - push back against the dominant "Green AI" narrative that focuses primarily on making models more computationally efficient. Their critical perspective suggests that systemic factors (scale of deployment, rebound effects, growth in AI usage) may overwhelm efficiency gains, echoing the Jevons paradox concern in digital technology sustainability.

## Key Data

- Published in Communications of the ACM (CACM) - high-profile venue for reaching AI practitioners
- Authors argue for "looking beyond efficiency" in sustainable AI
- Implies efficiency-first approach may be counterproductive or insufficient

## Critical Analysis

This paper represents an important counterpoint to the "Green AI" efficiency narrative:
- Most Green AI research focuses on reducing energy per inference/training
- This paper argues that framing is inadequate
- Likely addresses scale/growth as the dominant factor in AI environmental impact
- Aligns with Bieser et al. (2024) critique of ICT industry's "enablement" claims
