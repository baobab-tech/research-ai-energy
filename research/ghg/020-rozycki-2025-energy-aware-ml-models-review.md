# Energy-Aware Machine Learning Models: A Review of Recent Techniques and Perspectives

**Topic:** GHG Emissions - Efficiency Techniques
**Source:** Rozycki, Solarska & Waligora, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/en18112810

## Insight

This 2025 review from Poznan University of Technology provides a comprehensive analysis of techniques to reduce ML energy consumption while maintaining (or improving) model performance. Critically, the paper highlights a "pivotal yet often overlooked factor": the substantial emissions tied to ML hardware production. The authors argue that in many cases, embodied emissions from hardware manufacturing eclipse operational emissions - underscoring the need to optimize both deployment efficiency AND manufacturing processes for meaningful environmental impact.

## Key Data

- **Key techniques reviewed**: model compression, pruning, quantization, hardware design optimization
- **Critical finding**: Hardware production emissions often eclipse operational emissions in ML
- **Scope**: Energy use in large-scale ML models and environmental footprint
- **Call to action**: Dual-pronged strategy tackling both operational efficiency AND hardware production carbon intensity
- **10 citations** in 2025 suggests growing influence

## Key Insight: Embodied vs Operational Emissions

The paper's most important contribution is highlighting that:
- Embodied emissions (hardware production) may exceed operational emissions
- This shifts focus from just "efficient inference" to full lifecycle analysis
- Manufacturing process optimization is necessary for meaningful environmental impact
- Aligns with Jiang et al. (2024) lifecycle framework for LLM carbon analysis

## Dual Strategy Proposed

1. **Operational efficiency**: Model compression, pruning, quantization, energy-efficient deployment
2. **Manufacturing carbon intensity**: Hardware production process optimization

The authors argue both prongs are necessary - operational efficiency alone is insufficient.
