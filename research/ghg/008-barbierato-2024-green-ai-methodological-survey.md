# Green AI Survey: Hardware Manufacturing Impacts Deep Learning's Environmental Cost

**Topic:** GHG Emissions - Lifecycle/Embodied
**Source:** Barbierato & Gatti, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1109/access.2024.3360705

## Insight

This methodological survey of scientific literature on Green AI explicitly addresses hardware innovations as a key factor in AI's environmental footprint. The paper distinguishes between "Red AI" (resource-intensive, accuracy-focused) and "Green AI" (sustainability-focused) paradigms, covering not just training and inference energy but also hardware manufacturing considerations. The authors organize literature into clusters including hardware innovations for reducing ecological consequences of complex AI applications like GPT and BERT.

## Key Data

- Deep Learning deployment has "exorbitant levels of energy consumption" for training and inference
- Hardware innovations identified as one of three key areas for Green AI (alongside algorithms and data utilization)
- "Red AI" models undergo training using "resource-intensive methodologies on very large datasets"
- Unless complex training procedures and software models mitigate environmental impact, system accuracy must decrease
- 28 citations indicates emerging influence in Green AI discourse

## Notes

- Published in IEEE Access (open access, peer-reviewed)
- Authors from Italy
- Explicitly addresses the "Red AI vs Green AI" framework gaining traction in sustainability discourse
- Identifies compromise between sustainability and performance as core challenge
- Hardware manufacturing mentioned but specific lifecycle data not primary focus
- Gap: More detail on embodied emissions ratios would strengthen lifecycle analysis
