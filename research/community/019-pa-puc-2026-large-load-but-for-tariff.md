# Pennsylvania's PUC assigned network upgrade costs to large loads on a "but-for" test; the state's utility trade group petitioned for reconsideration and reserved federal constitutional claims

**Topic:** Cost allocation — who pays for interconnection and who bears stranded-asset risk; and what the utilities themselves argue
**Source:** Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission, Docket M-2025-3054271 (Final Order, 12 May 2026); Energy Association of Pennsylvania, Petition for Reconsideration or Clarification (27 May 2026)
**Type:** regulatory order and docket filing
**URL:** https://www.puc.pa.gov/pcdocs/1932400.pdf
**Source note:** EAP petition, quoting the Final Order); press release: https://www.puc.pa.gov/press-release/2026/puc-releases-final-order-establishing-first-of-its-kind-large-load-model-tariff-framework-05132026
**Published:** 2026-05-12 (order entered); 2026-05-27 (petition filed)

## Finding

The Pennsylvania PUC adopted a model tariff for customers above 50 MW individually or 100 MW in aggregate that allocates network upgrade costs on a strict causation rule: "If a Network Improvement would not have been needed 'but for' the interconnection of the Large load Customer, then the costs of the upgrade would be allocated to that customer irrespective of whether other customers would benefit from it." This replaced the weaker majority-of-benefit test in the Commission's November 2025 Tentative Order. Within fifteen days, the Energy Association of Pennsylvania — the trade body for the state's electric and gas distribution companies — petitioned for reconsideration, arguing the test intrudes on FERC's exclusive jurisdiction over transmission, and expressly reserved the right to sue in federal court under the Federal Power Act, the Commerce Clause, the Supremacy Clause, the Fourteenth Amendment and Section 1983 of the Civil Rights Act. PPL Electric Utilities declined to join the petition.

## Methodology

Primary regulatory documents, not estimates. The Commission's stated reasoning, as quoted by EAP: "Given the unprecedented nature of this load growth, we find that the Commission's approach to cost allocation must adapt to shield ratepayers from socialization of costs that are properly attributed to Large Load Customers." The Commission also grounded the test in the statement of principles agreed by the governors of the thirteen PJM states, and cited NRDC's argument that state jurisdiction over retail interconnection holds regardless of whether the connection is to distribution or transmission.

## Limitations and conflicts

The EAP petition is an adversarial filing by the regulated utilities and should be read as advocacy; its jurisdictional argument rests on the Federal Power Act's division between transmission (16 USC § 824(b), FERC) and local distribution (states), and on the FERC Order 888 seven-factor test. Whether the PUC's rule survives is unresolved as of the retrieval date.

The order is a *model* tariff providing "guidance"; individual EDC tariffs still require separate approval, so the operative terms at any given utility may differ. Numerical values for minimum contract term, minimum take percentage, collateral per MW and the exit fee formula are in the Final Order itself, which is not summarised numerically in the press release or in the petition; this excerpt therefore records only the provisions that are directly quoted or stated. Do not cite specific dollar or percentage figures for Pennsylvania from this file.

The deeper limitation is that a but-for cost-allocation rule addresses the *interconnection* cost shift, not the *commodity and capacity* cost shift documented in the PJM capacity market — those are FERC-jurisdictional wholesale costs that a state CIAC rule does not reach.
