# Growing Waste in HPC-enabled Academic Practice

**Topic:** Community and Grid Impact - Computing Demand and Growth
**Source:** Lord et al., 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713919

## Insight

This CHI 2025 paper documents how research institutions (universities, conferences, funders) systematically "reinforce and embed, rather than challenge, expectations of growth and waste" in computing infrastructure. The study reveals that generative AI and ever-larger models are "multiplying" demand, but academic and research cultures lack mechanisms to de-escalate this consumption. This is directly relevant to community impacts because academic computing represents a significant and growing share of overall data center demand.

## Key Data

- 25 interviews conducted with researchers, HPC providers, funders, and commissioners
- Identifies "intersecting socio-cultural and technical dimensions" that perpetuate waste
- Calls for "de-escalating demand" and "more moderate, responsible and meaningful use" of computing
- Notes that despite known environmental impacts of ICT globally, "little consideration has been given" to limiting research innovation impacts

## Critical Note

The paper frames the issue as cultural as much as technical - research institutions reward computing growth rather than efficiency. This suggests that data center demand will continue growing unless incentive structures change fundamentally.

## Quote

> "At a time when large scale cloud systems, generative AI and ever larger models are multiplying, we argue to de-escalate demand for computing, aiming for more moderate, responsible and meaningful use of computational infrastructures."
