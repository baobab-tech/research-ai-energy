# Greening China's Digital Economy: East-West Computing Resources Transmission

**Topic:** Community Impact - Grid/Ratepayer
**Source:** Xie, Han, and Tan, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-024-02963-0

## Insight

China's East-West Computing Resources Transmission (EWCRT) Project demonstrates both the massive scale of data center infrastructure expansion and the intentional strategy to shift energy-intensive computing to regions with cheaper renewable resources. The project consists of 8 data center hubs and 10 clusters across China, illustrating how governments are actively directing data center siting to manage grid impacts. Estimated CO2 reductions of 2,125-9,500 Mt by 2050 depend on PUE improvements and green electricity adoption, revealing the enormous energy footprint being planned for.

## Key Data

- **Infrastructure scale:** 8 national data center hubs, 10 regional clusters across China
- **Grid strategy:** Shifts computing load from eastern coastal population centers to western regions with renewable energy surplus
- **CO2 reduction potential:** 2,125 Mt (base case) to 9,500 Mt (with PUE and green energy improvements) by 2050
- **Implicit ratepayer impact:** Eastern consumers subsidize transmission infrastructure; western regions bear local environmental burden
- **Who benefits:** Tech companies gain access to cheaper electricity; question of community benefit distribution unaddressed

## Relevance to Ratepayer Impacts

The EWCRT model shows how data center expansion creates cross-regional infrastructure dependencies. Transmission costs for moving data center compute to remote renewable-rich areas are typically socialized across ratepayers, while benefits accrue to tech companies. The paper notes need for "differentiated strategies" for different data center types, implying one-size-fits-all approaches to grid integration are inadequate.
