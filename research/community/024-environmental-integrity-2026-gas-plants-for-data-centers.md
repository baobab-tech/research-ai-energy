# 74 gas plants proposed to serve US data centres would add 143 GW and 662 Mt CO2e/yr; 88% sit in counties with below-average life expectancy

**Topic:** Siting and environmental justice — dedicated fossil generation built for data centres and who lives next to it
**Source:** Environmental Integrity Project, "The Power Behind AI", 2026
**Type:** NGO report built on permit-application data
**URL:** https://environmentalintegrity.org/news/74-gas-power-plants-proposed-for-u-s-data-centers-could-release-as-much-climate-pollution-as-australia/
**Source note:** report: https://environmentalintegrity.org/reports/the-power-behind-ai/
**Published:** 2026-07-01

## Finding

The Environmental Integrity Project identified 74 gas-fired power plant projects (71 new, 3 expansions) proposed specifically to serve data centres, totalling 143 GW. Aggregating the emissions figures in their air-permit applications gives 662 million tons CO2e/yr — comparable to Australia's national emissions — plus 159,142 tons/yr of criteria pollutants, of which 44,281 tons NOx and 32,684 tons PM2.5. The demographic overlay is the part that bears on siting equity: of the 562,825 people living within three miles of 67 of these plants, 223,489 (40 percent) are in low-income households, and 62 of 70 plants sit in counties where life expectancy is below the 77-year national average. 188 schools fall within three miles of a proposed plant.

## Methodology

Permit-application aggregation. EIP maintains the Oil & Gas Watch database of pending and issued air permits; the emissions figures are the potential-to-emit values stated in those applications, summed. This is a ceiling, not a projection: proposed plants frequently do not get built, capacity factors are typically far below permitted maximums, and the 662 Mt figure assumes every project proceeds and runs at permitted levels. The demographic analysis is a three-mile buffer against census data, which is a coarse proxy for exposure and does not model dispersion or prevailing winds.

## Limitations and conflicts

EIP is an advocacy organisation founded by former EPA enforcement attorneys; the report ends with policy recommendations (clean generation, best-available pollution control, dry cooling), so it is written to a conclusion. The underlying object — permit applications — is public and checkable, which is the report's strength.

The attribution "proposed for data centers" is EIP's classification and its criteria are not visible in the press summary; some plants serving general load growth may be included, and conversely some data-centre load will be served by existing plants not counted here. The low-income and life-expectancy correlations are ecological (county and three-mile-buffer level), so they establish co-location, not causation or disproportionate siting relative to a counterfactual — gas plants have long been sited near industrial land and cheap property, so the finding is that data-centre generation follows that existing pattern rather than that it creates a new one.
