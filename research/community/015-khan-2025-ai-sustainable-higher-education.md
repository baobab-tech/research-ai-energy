# AI for Sustainable Higher Education: Environmental Tradeoffs

**Topic:** Community and Grid Impact - Institutional AI Environmental Footprint
**Source:** Khan et al., 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s43621-025-00809-6

## Insight

This paper examines how universities deploying AI face a dual imperative: "technological advancement and environmental imperatives." While AI can "improve educational outcomes, resource management, and long-term sustainability," its deployment also requires significant energy infrastructure. The paper emphasizes the need for "strong ethical frameworks" and "robust institutional rules and infrastructure" - relevant to communities hosting educational data centers. Universities represent a significant and growing segment of computing demand.

## Key Data

- AI in higher education can "minimize environmental impact" and "enhance energy efficiency" when properly deployed
- However, AI deployment requires institutional infrastructure investment
- Paper addresses "digital divide" - relevant to equity impacts on communities
- Identifies tension between AI innovation and sustainability goals
- 30 citations as of early 2025

## Critical Note

The paper presents AI as potentially beneficial for sustainability without quantifying the energy costs of AI deployment in educational settings. The "digital divide" concern is mentioned but not connected to data center siting and community impacts. This reflects a common gap in the literature: institutional benefits are discussed, but infrastructure costs borne by local communities are not.

## Quote

> "As higher education faces technological advancement and environmental imperatives, AI becomes a key instrument for revolutionizing instructional methods and institutional operations."
