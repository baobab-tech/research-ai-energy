# Global Energy Crisis and Energy Efficiency: Impact on Ratepayers

**Topic:** Community Impact - Grid/Ratepayer
**Source:** Gajdzik, Wolniak, Nagaj, Zuromskaite, and Grebski, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/en17040947

## Insight

The global energy crisis that began in 2021 (post-pandemic recovery) and intensified with Russia's Ukraine invasion in 2022 fundamentally changed energy market conditions. This comprehensive analysis documents how record-high natural gas and electricity prices pushed households into energy poverty, forced factories to cut production or close, and slowed economic growth. The crisis context is directly relevant to data center expansion: adding massive new industrial loads during a period of constrained supply and elevated prices exacerbates pressures on residential ratepayers.

## Key Data

- **Price records:** Natural gas and electricity prices reached record levels; oil prices hit highest since 2008
- **Social impact:** High energy prices "pushed many families into poverty" - direct household burden
- **Industrial impact:** Some factories forced to "cut production or even close" due to energy costs
- **Economic impact:** Energy crisis contributed to recession conditions in some countries
- **Paradox:** Crisis may accelerate renewable energy adoption but creates short-term hardship

## Ratepayer Relevance

This paper provides the macroeconomic context for understanding why data center energy demand is particularly contentious. When residential consumers and industrial factories are facing energy price crises, adding hundreds of megawatts of new data center load to already-stressed grids intensifies competition for finite electricity supply. The paper's finding that households are being pushed into energy poverty while energy-intensive industries demand more capacity frames the equity question: should residential ratepayers subsidize infrastructure for data centers during an energy crisis?
