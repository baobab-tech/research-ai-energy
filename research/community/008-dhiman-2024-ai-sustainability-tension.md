# AI and Sustainability: The Fundamental Tension

**Topic:** Community Impact - Grid/Ratepayer
**Source:** Dhiman et al., 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/analytics3010008

## Insight

This comprehensive review identifies a fundamental tension in AI sustainability discourse: "Sustainability of AI" versus "AI for Sustainability." While industry narratives focus on AI's potential benefits for climate solutions, the paper documents persistent concerns about AI's own substantial energy consumption. The review found a surge in empirical publications since 2019, signaling growing scientific scrutiny of AI's environmental footprint rather than acceptance of industry claims.

## Key Data

- **Research bifurcation:** Literature divides into "Sustainability of AI" (examining AI's footprint) and "AI for Sustainability" (examining AI's beneficial applications)
- **Publication trend:** Surge in empirical studies since 2019 signals field maturation and increased scrutiny
- **Dimensions:** Economic, social, and environmental sustainability all identified as concerns, with economic dimension under-explored
- **Energy consumption:** Explicitly identified as persistent concern despite AI sector's sustainability claims
- **Research gap:** Social equity implications of AI energy consumption underrepresented in literature

## Ratepayer Relevance

The "Sustainability of AI" framework directly addresses the community impact question: who bears the costs of AI's energy consumption? The paper's finding that the economic dimension remains "relatively under-explored" suggests insufficient attention to how AI energy costs are distributed across ratepayers, communities, and nations. The call for aligning research with UN Sustainable Development Goals implies current AI deployment patterns may conflict with energy equity objectives.
