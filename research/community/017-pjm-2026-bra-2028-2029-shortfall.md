# PJM's 2028/2029 capacity auction cleared at the price cap and 6,831 MW short of its own reliability requirement, the second consecutive shortfall

**Topic:** Grid impact — generation adequacy and price formation under data-centre-driven load growth
**Source:** PJM Interconnection, 2026
**Type:** grid-operator primary report
**URL:** https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/markets-ops/rpm/rpm-auction-info/2028-2029/2028-2029-bra-results-report.pdf
**Published:** 2026-07-14

## Finding

The 2028/2029 Base Residual Auction cleared every locational deliverability area at the administrative price cap of $325.00/MW-day and still procured 6,831.3 MW UCAP less than PJM's reliability requirement — a 14.4 percent installed reserve margin against a 20.0 percent target. PJM attributes the increase in the reliability requirement (152,400 to 156,013 MW UCAP) largely to "additional Large Loads." The product of cleared megawatts and clearing price was $16.4 billion, unchanged from the prior auction only because the cap bound in both. PJM's own appendix simulation removing the cap and floor puts the unconstrained clearing price at $554.72/MW-day RTO-wide and $776.69 in the ComEd zone, worth $29.7 billion — $13.3 billion above the capped result.

## Methodology

Auction settlement data, not estimate. The one modelled quantity is the no-cap/no-floor simulation in the appendix, which re-clears the same offers against the unconstrained VRR curve. The shortfall is a direct comparison of procured UCAP against the administratively determined reliability requirement.

## Limitations and conflicts

PJM is the entity whose market design is under criticism and whose load forecast drives the demand curve; it has an institutional interest in framing the shortfall as a supply problem rather than a forecast-quality or large-load-interconnection problem. The report's one editorial sentence — "A price cap below the established VRR Curve can reduce the amount of investment, and therefore supply, in the PJM region" — is an argument against the Pennsylvania settlement, not a finding.

The document does not decompose load growth into data centres versus electrification versus organic growth; "Large Loads" is PJM's term and covers both. Only 524.7 MW of genuinely new generation cleared, which undercuts the premise that high prices are calling forth supply. PJM scheduled a one-off Reliability Backstop Auction for September 2026 to fill the gap, the cost of which is not in the $16.4 billion.
