# Making AI Less 'Thirsty': The Secret Water Footprint of AI Models

**Topic:** Community Impact - Water / Environmental Justice
**Source:** Li, Yang, Islam, Ren, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3724499

## Insight

This 2025 Communications of the ACM paper by the UC Riverside / UT Arlington team is the authoritative peer-reviewed source on AI's "secret water footprint." Building on their earlier work, the authors document how AI model training and inference consume substantial water resources through data center cooling, creating direct competition with residential and agricultural water needs in water-stressed regions. The paper calls for transparency and accounting of AI's water consumption - a direct community impact issue.

## Key Data

- **GPT-3 training:** Evaporated approximately 700,000 liters of water
- **ChatGPT inference:** Approximately 500 mL (a standard water bottle) per 20-50 queries
- **Per-query estimate:** 10-25 mL of water per ChatGPT response
- **Global projection:** AI could consume 4.2-6.6 billion cubic meters of water by 2027
- **Geographic variation:** Water footprint varies significantly by data center location and cooling methods

## Community Impact Relevance

The water consumption findings have direct community implications: data centers in water-stressed regions (Arizona, Nevada, Chile) compete with residential and agricultural users for finite water resources. The paper's emphasis on "uncovering" the "secret" water footprint highlights the transparency gap that prevents communities from understanding the true resource demands of AI infrastructure in their regions. This is a 2025 update to the foundational work, now peer-reviewed in a top computing venue.

## Methodological Note

This is the peer-reviewed CACM version of the researchers' earlier arXiv preprint. The CACM publication provides additional credibility and represents the most authoritative current estimate of AI water consumption. The authors are from UC Riverside and UT Arlington - academic researchers with no obvious industry conflicts of interest.
