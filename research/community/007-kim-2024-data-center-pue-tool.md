# Data Center Energy Evaluation Tool: PUE Analysis Across Climate Zones

**Topic:** Community Impact - Grid/Ratepayer
**Source:** Kim, Shin, and Kim, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings14010299

## Insight

Data centers are energy-intensive facilities where over 95% of cooling load comes from IT equipment heat generation. This paper's analysis of Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) across climate zones reveals that data center efficiency varies significantly by location (PUE ranges from 1.15 to 1.43), with implications for local grid burden. The wide PUE range means identical computing loads can require 25% more grid power in some locations than others, directly impacting local electricity infrastructure requirements and potentially rates.

## Key Data

- **Energy intensity:** Over 95% of data center cooling load attributed to IT equipment heat
- **PUE variation:** Ranges from 1.15 (best case with evaporative cooling) to 1.43 (indirect air-side economizer in humid climates)
- **Climate dependency:** Dry zones achieve PUE reductions of 0.02-0.05 with evaporative cooling; wet zones see 0.03-0.07 improvements with indirect air-side economizers
- **Grid implications:** A data center with PUE 1.43 vs 1.15 requires 24% more grid electricity for identical computing output
- **Infrastructure cost:** Higher PUE locations require larger substation capacity, transmission infrastructure

## Ratepayer Relevance

The substantial variation in data center efficiency by climate zone means communities in less optimal locations face disproportionate grid infrastructure costs. When data centers site in humid regions (PUE 1.43), local utilities must provide significantly more power capacity than if the same facility were in a dry climate zone (PUE 1.15). These infrastructure costs are typically passed to all ratepayers in the service territory.
