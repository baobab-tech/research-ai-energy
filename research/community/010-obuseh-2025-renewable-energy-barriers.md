# Barriers to Renewable Energy Integration: Grid Infrastructure and Social Acceptance

**Topic:** Community Impact - Grid Strain / Energy Justice
**Source:** Obuseh, Eyenubo, Alele, Okpare, Ikponmwosa, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.24112/jaes.090002

## Insight

This 2025 systematic review analyzes technical, economic, social, environmental, and policy barriers hindering renewable energy adoption across Africa, Asia, Europe, and the Americas. The findings directly inform data center community impacts: the same grid infrastructure challenges that impede renewable energy integration (intermittency, storage limitations, smart grid requirements) are exacerbated when large new industrial loads like data centers are added. The paper identifies "socio-cultural resistance" and "aesthetic concerns" as key social barriers - themes highly relevant to community opposition against data center projects.

## Key Data

- **Grid infrastructure gaps:** Need for more accurate forecasting tools, scalable energy storage systems, and smart grid infrastructure
- **Economic barriers:** High initial capital costs, insufficient financial mechanisms, and socio-economic disparities
- **Social barriers:** Cultural resistance, aesthetic concerns, and lack of public engagement are significant barriers
- **Environmental barriers:** Lifecycle impacts and biodiversity concerns must be addressed
- **Policy barriers:** Regulatory fragmentation and inadequate governance frameworks impede progress
- **Regional disparities:** Barriers vary significantly across Africa, Asia, Europe, and the Americas

## Community Impact Relevance

When data centers locate in regions already struggling with grid infrastructure and renewable integration challenges, they compound existing problems. The paper's identification of "socio-economic disparities" as a barrier to energy transition parallels environmental justice concerns: communities with weaker grid infrastructure and fewer financial resources bear disproportionate burdens. The recommendation for "inclusive financing mechanisms" and "harmonized policy frameworks" applies equally to data center siting decisions.

## Quote

> "Addressing these gaps requires targeted solutions, such as AI-driven forecasting systems, hybrid energy storage technologies, inclusive financing mechanisms, and harmonized policy frameworks."
