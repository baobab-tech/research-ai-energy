# xAI ran 33 unpermitted gas turbines for its Memphis-area data centre in a region already failing the ozone standard; NAACP's Clean Air Act suit puts potential NOx emissions at 2,508 tons/year

**Topic:** Environmental justice and local air quality — on-site generation for AI data centres, permitting, and the communities exposed
**Source:** NAACP and Mississippi State Conference NAACP v. xAI Corp. and MZX Tech, represented by the Southern Environmental Law Center and Earthjustice; N.D. Miss. Case No. 3:26-cv-00074-MPM-JMV
**Type:** litigation filing / primary legal document
**URL:** https://earthjustice.org/press/2026/naacp-asks-court-for-emergency-action-to-stop-illegal-air-pollution-from-xais-data-center-power-plant
**Source note:** preliminary injunction brief: https://earthjustice.org/document/xai-preliminary-injunction-brief ; https://www.selc.org/press-release/civil-rights-group-sues-xai-for-illegal-pollution-from-data-center-power-plant/
**Published:** complaint filed 2026-04-14; emergency motion 2026-05-06

## Finding

xAI installed and operated gas turbines to power its Colossus 2 data centre in Southaven, Mississippi, across the state line from South Memphis, without a Clean Air Act preconstruction permit. The count rose from 27 turbines at the time of the February 2026 notice of intent to sue to 33 by the May 2026 emergency motion — six more added after the company was formally notified it was operating illegally. Plaintiffs' potential-to-emit calculations put the installation at 2,508 tons/year of NOx, which would make it the largest industrial NOx source in the Memphis airshed, an area already in non-attainment for ozone. This is the clearest documented case of an AI data centre bypassing air permitting to secure power, and it sits in a majority-Black metropolitan area that the American Lung Association graded "F" for ozone in both Shelby County, Tennessee and DeSoto County, Mississippi.

## Methodology

Potential-to-emit figures are engineering calculations from turbine nameplate ratings and emission factors assuming maximum permitted operation, not stack-test measurements or monitored ambient concentrations. Potential to emit is the regulatory trigger for major-source permitting under the Clean Air Act, so it is the legally relevant quantity, but it is an upper bound on actual emissions and should never be reported as measured output. No ambient monitoring data attributing measured Memphis-area concentrations to this facility has been produced in the filings reviewed.

## Limitations and conflicts

Plaintiffs' filings are adversarial documents and the emissions figures are their calculations, not a regulator's or the company's. xAI's own position is not captured here. The case was unresolved at the retrieval date; a preliminary injunction motion had been filed but no ruling is recorded in these sources. The jump from 1,700 to 2,508 tons/year of NOx between the April and May filings tracks the turbine count increase from 27 to 33, which is consistent, but the two figures should not be used interchangeably.

Note also the split jurisdiction: the turbines are in Mississippi (MDEQ permitting), the exposed population is concentrated in Tennessee (Shelby County Health Department), and a separate appeal of the Shelby County turbine permits for the original Memphis Colossus site is proceeding independently. Neither regulator has authority over the other's airshed effects, which is itself the structural finding.
