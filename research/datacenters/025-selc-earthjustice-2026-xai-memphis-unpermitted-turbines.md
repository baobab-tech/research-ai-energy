# xAI built roughly 1.2 GW of on-site gas generation for its Memphis AI campus and ran dozens of turbines without air permits; DOJ intervened on national-security grounds in June 2026

**Topic:** Data-centre infrastructure — power sourcing, behind-the-meter gas for AI campuses
**Source:** Southern Environmental Law Center; Earthjustice (litigation record, NAACP v. xAI and related proceedings), 2025–2026
**Type:** litigation record / NGO case documentation (advocacy parties — see conflicts)

**Published:** case pages created 2026-04-14, updated through 2026-06

## Finding

xAI's Memphis-area AI campuses are powered substantially by on-site methane gas turbines installed ahead of, and in place of, grid interconnection. The litigation record describes 35 turbines at Colossus 1 — 33 observed operating by thermal imaging — installed and run without Clean Air Act permits, later replaced by 15 permanent turbines for which permits were obtained after a notice of intent to sue; and 27 turbines at Colossus 2 in Mississippi, up to 495 MW, also operating unpermitted. Combined on-site generation at the Southaven site is described as approximately 1.2 GW. Potential annual emissions from the unpermitted Colossus 2 turbines are stated as more than 1,700 tons of NOx, up to 180 tons of fine particulate matter, 500 tons of carbon monoxide, and 19 tons of formaldehyde.

The regulatory sequence is the substantive finding. In August 2024 the Shelby County Health Department declined jurisdiction over the operating turbines and referred the matter to EPA. EPA subsequently ruled that such turbines require permits before they may run. In June 2026 the US Department of Justice filed a brief supporting xAI's continued operation, citing national security. Permitting exposure was resolved by exemption and intervention, not by the plant stopping.

## Methodology

Facts are drawn from litigation filings, agency correspondence and SELC's own aerial and thermal-imaging surveys of the sites. Emissions figures are *potential to emit* — the regulatory construct of maximum permissible annual emissions at design capacity — not measured stack emissions. Actual emissions depend on run hours and are not reported.

## Limitations and conflicts

Both sources are litigants or allied advocacy organisations arguing the case; every figure here is an adversarial party's characterisation. The underlying permits, the Shelby County Health Department permit numbers and conditions, the EPA rule text, and the DOJ brief were not retrieved directly — epw.senate.gov, Reuters and selc.org all refused automated retrieval, and the permits are not posted in machine-readable form. Turbine counts are inconsistent across sources (SELC describes 62 turbines across both campuses; a separate report claims 59 unpermitted turbines at Colossus 2 against the 27 in the Earthjustice filing), and no count here is independently confirmed. xAI's own position is not represented because the company has not published one. Treat capacity figures as indicative and emissions figures as regulatory potential-to-emit, and source any quantitative claim to the permit documents before publication.

## Relation to existing corpus

On-site generating capacity at the Memphis campus is about 1.2 GW. The sequence at Colossus 1 ran
35 unpermitted temporary turbines, later replaced by 15 permitted permanent units. Planned grid
connection covers 300 MW against 1,200 MW of on-site generation. The DOJ intervened on xAI's side
in June 2026 on national-security grounds.

Emissions and air-quality figures for this dispute are in
`../community/022-naacp-xai-2026-memphis-turbines-clean-air-act.md`, which draws on the complaint
(N.D. Miss. 3:26-cv-00074-MPM-JMV) and the preliminary-injunction brief.

This is the concrete counter-case to `013-rollinson-2025-offgrid-hyperscale-feasibility.md`, which
models off-grid hybrid renewables as beating the grid on cost and emissions. The largest off-grid
AI campus built to date chose unabated gas, sited in a community with pre-existing air-quality
burden, and the binding constraint proved to be permitting rather than economics.
