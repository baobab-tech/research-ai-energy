# Data Center PUE Varies Significantly by Climate Zone and Cooling Strategy

**Topic:** Data Centers - Power Sources
**Source:** Kim, Shin & Kim, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings14010299

## Insight

This study develops an energy evaluation tool for data centers and reveals that Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) varies dramatically based on climate zone and economizer strategy. Over 95% of data center cooling load comes from heat generated by IT equipment. The research shows PUE ranging from 1.15 to 1.43 depending on location and cooling approach - meaning that identical workloads in different regions or with different infrastructure can consume 20-30% more total energy. This has direct implications for evaluating corporate claims about data center efficiency: a company operating in dry climates with optimal cooling can claim much better energy efficiency than one in humid regions, even with identical IT operations.

## Key Data

- 95%+ of cooling load attributed to IT equipment heat
- PUE ranges observed: 1.17-1.39 (DASE systems), 1.15-1.17 (DASE+EC systems)
- IASE + EC systems: PUE 1.22-1.43 in humid zones (0A, 0B, 1A, 1B, 2A)
- Evaporative cooling reduces PUE by 0.02-0.05 in dry zones, 0.03-0.07 in wet zones
- CRAH supply temperature and temperature difference are most influential PUE factors

## Notes

- Methodology: Tool development verified against DesignBuilder/EnergyPlus algorithms
- Published in Buildings journal (peer-reviewed)
- Authors from Korean institutions
- Key insight: regional climate significantly affects achievable efficiency
- Implications: corporate PUE claims must be contextualized by location
