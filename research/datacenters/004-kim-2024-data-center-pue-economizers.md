# Simulated annual PUE for a 10 MW air-cooled data hall spans 1.16 to 1.43 across 15 climate zones and four air-side economizer configurations

**Topic:** Data-centre infrastructure — how much of a PUE figure is climate and cooling design rather than operator performance
**Source:** Kim, J.H. (Kwangwoon University); Shin, D.U. (Kunsan National University); Kim, H. (Plant Research Group, POSCO E&C), 2024
**Type:** peer-reviewed (simulation study)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings14010299
**Source note:** Open-access PDF retrieved from https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/buildings/buildings-14-00299/article_deploy/buildings-14-00299.pdf
**Published:** 2024-01

## Finding

A validated hourly simulation of one reference data hall, held identical in IT load and equipment and moved across all 15 ASHRAE climate zones with four air-side economizer configurations, produces annual PUE between 1.16 and 1.43. The same facility therefore reports roughly a quarter more total electricity per unit of computing in its worst climate-and-cooling combination than in its best, before any difference in operator practice. Within a zone, the choice of economizer moves PUE by an order comparable to the choice of site: adding evaporative cooling cuts PUE by 0.02 to 0.05 in dry zones, and substituting an indirect for a direct air-side economizer cuts it by 0.03 to 0.07 in wet zones.

The study also identifies which design parameters the number is most sensitive to. CRAH supply temperature and CRAH temperature difference dominate annual PUE, and adjusting those two alone moves PUE by 0.08 to 0.12 in cold zones (6A and above).

## Methodology

The authors built DCSim, an hourly data-centre energy tool implementing EnergyPlus algorithms, and validated it against DesignBuilder v7.0.2.4 on a 10 MW ITE reference facility, comparing per-equipment hourly power. CVRMSE fell within ASHRAE Guideline 14 criteria. They then ran the validated tool over TMY weather for one representative city per ASHRAE climate zone, with four configurations: direct air-side economizer (DASE), DASE plus evaporative cooling, indirect air-side economizer (IASE), and IASE plus evaporative cooling. Air-to-air heat exchanger effectiveness was set to 0.7 and evaporative cooling effectiveness to 0.6, both from the EnergyPlus engineering reference. Sensitivity used a two-level full factorial design over six cooling-medium temperature factors; uncertainty was generated by dividing the two dominant factors into 11 levels each, producing 121 cases per economizer system per zone.

Every one of the 15 representative cities is in Asia: Bangkok, Abu Dhabi, Hanoi, Kuwait, Taipei, Wuhan, Tehran, Incheon, Tabriz, Sapporo, Xinjiang, Changchun, Hohhot, Semipalatinsk and Chita.

## Limitations and conflicts

Conflict disclosed in the source: co-author Heegang Kim is employed by Plant Research Group, POSCO E&C, an engineering and construction firm that builds industrial plant and data-centre facilities, and POSCO E&C is acknowledged as providing financial support. The remaining funding is public (National Research Foundation of Korea grant 2021R1G1A1094364; Kwangwoon University 2023-0115). The commercial interest is in a design tool that a construction firm would use to bid facility efficiency, which is a reason to treat the tool's favourable validation with care but does not bear on the direction of the climate result.

Every PUE value is modelled, not measured, and the tool is validated against another model rather than against a metered facility. The scope is air-cooled data halls with CRAH-supplied air; the authors state the method does not apply to liquid-cooled facilities, which is the configuration AI training clusters increasingly use. All 15 zones are represented by Asian cities, so the zone results carry the diurnal and humidity character of those specific sites rather than of the zone in general.

Two of the source's headline ranges are frequently misread. The bands 1.17–1.39, 1.15–1.17, 1.22–1.43 and 1.17–1.43 quoted in the abstract are uncertainty bands produced by sweeping two CRAH design parameters within named high-uncertainty zones. They are not the across-climate PUE range. The authors state their own across-climate spectrum as 1.16 to 1.43.

The "over 95% of cooling load from IT heat" claim is not a result of this study. It is a secondhand citation to a single 2007 Korean-language study of internet data centres. The figure is close to definitional for an air-cooled data hall of this power density, where envelope, lighting and occupancy loads are small against 10 MW of IT heat, and this model assumes exactly that by fixing other loads at 0.054 kW/m². It should not be presented as an independently measured finding.
