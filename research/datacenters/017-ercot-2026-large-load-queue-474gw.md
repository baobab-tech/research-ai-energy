# ERCOT holds 474 GW of large-load interconnection requests, ~90% data centres, but only ~205 GW clears the first eligibility screen

**Topic:** Data-centre infrastructure — the gap between announced and studied capacity in a single grid region
**Source:** Pablo Vegas / ERCOT, presentation to the Texas Senate Committee on Business & Commerce, 2026
**Type:** grid-operator filing (public legislative testimony deck)
**URL:** https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/07/29/ERCOT-Senate-July-29-Panel-1-Assessing-The-Grid.pdf
**Published:** 2026-07-29 (queue data as of June 2026; Batch Zero eligibility data as of 2026-07-28)

## Finding

ERCOT is tracking approximately 474 GW of large loads seeking interconnection as of June 2026, of which about 90% are data centres. ERCOT's all-time system peak is under 90 GW, so requested large-load capacity is roughly five times the entire existing grid peak. The same deck quantifies the attrition: under the Senate Bill 6 "Batch Zero" process, only about 205 GW of that was preliminarily eligible for the first batch study. 274 GW of requests (315 projects) had no qualifying interconnection study at all, and a further 20 GW (47 projects) were removed for failing to submit dynamic model data by the 10 July 2026 deadline. This is a grid operator's own arithmetic on the difference between a request and a project — before construction, before energisation, and before any electron is delivered.

## Methodology

Administrative counts from ERCOT's own interconnection processes, not estimates or models. Large-load figures are the aggregate of requests submitted by Interconnecting Large Load Entities through transmission/distribution service providers. The Batch Zero eligibility split is ERCOT's preliminary classification pending final determination. ERCOT states it will conduct verification of a sample of applicants between 2026-07-10 and 2027-04-09, requiring documentary proof of attestations — purchase orders for long-lead equipment, real estate agreements, land-use approvals, end-user agreements and construction agreements — and that loads failing to demonstrate eligibility will be disqualified. That verification step is the mechanism by which the 205 GW figure should be expected to fall further.

## Limitations and conflicts

ERCOT is the counterparty to every number here and has an institutional interest in demonstrating that SB6 is working. The 474 GW figure is a count of requests, and a single project may appear in multiple interconnection requests across sites or developers; ERCOT does not publish a de-duplication factor. The 205 GW eligibility figure is explicitly labelled preliminary. The three eligible-category GW values (114/25/65) are read from a stacked bar chart, and the deck does not label which value maps to which project category, so only their sum (~205 GW) should be treated as firm. Nothing in the deck reports how much large load is *energised* today, which is the number that would close the announcement-to-delivery loop. Texas-only; ERCOT's energy-only market and fast interconnection make it the most permissive large-load queue in the US, so the ratio here is an upper bound on speculative requests, not a national average.
