# US Small and Midsize Data Centers: CBECS Survey Insights

**Topic:** Data Centers - Infrastructure and Efficiency
**Source:** Lei, Ganeshalingam, Masanet, Smith & Shehabi, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enbuild.2025.115734

## Insight

While hyperscale cloud facilities dominate efficiency discussions, this study reveals that small and midsize data centers in the US remain a "critical yet underexamined segment" with significant untapped energy savings potential. Using CBECS (Commercial Buildings Energy Consumption Survey) data from 2012-2018, the researchers document a sharp decline in small data centers (from 1.764 million to 1.398 million facilities) and server counts (from 5.177 million to 4.262 million), aligning with the broader shift to cloud computing. Critically, the study identifies a PUE-WUE tradeoff: midsize data centers are more energy-efficient but more water-intensive due to widespread use of water-cooled chillers.

## Key Data

- Small data center decline: 1.764M to 1.398M facilities (2012-2018)
- Server count decline: 5.177M to 4.262M servers
- Over 40% of servers housed in small/midsize data centers
- 55% of servers in midsize data centers
- Servers concentrated in climate zones 5A (cold), 3A (mixed-humid), 4A (mixed-humid)
- Highest densities in metropolitan hubs
- Direct expansion cooling still dominant, but transition to air economizers evident
- Key finding: PUE and WUE trade-off - more energy-efficient = more water-intensive
