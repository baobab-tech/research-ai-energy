# Data Center Energy Efficiency and Thermal Environment: Literature Review

**Topic:** Data Centers - Efficiency Measurement
**Source:** Setyo, Rijal, Aqilah & Abdullah, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/en18143689

## Insight

This literature review of 39 papers and 10 websites identifies systematic gaps in how data center energy efficiency is measured and reported. Key finding: the average IT power usage across studies was only 44.8% of total power, with an average PUE of 2.23 - significantly worse than the 1.2-1.4 PUE commonly claimed by hyperscalers. The review notes that existing literature "often overlooks categorizing equipment for power usage effectiveness (PUE), addressing power efficiency measurement limitations and employee thermal comfort." Passive cooling was found most applicable to larger-scale facilities (Hyperscale, Colocation) rather than smaller data centers.

## Key Data

- Papers reviewed: 39 papers + 10 websites
- Average IT power usage: 44.8% of total facility power
- Average PUE: 2.23 (compared to hyperscaler claims of 1.1-1.4)
- Indoor air temperatures averaged: 16.5C with 19% relative humidity
- Temperatures within ASHRAE allowable range
- Passive cooling: most effective for Hyperscale/Colocation facilities
- Gap identified: employee thermal comfort underexplored

## Notes

- Published July 2025
- 1 citation (newly published)
- Authors from Japan and Malaysia (Tokyo City University, Universiti Putra Malaysia)
- Critical finding: Average PUE of 2.23 is much higher than industry-reported averages
- This suggests selection bias in efficiency reporting - hyperscalers report, smaller facilities don't
- The 44.8% IT power usage means over half of data center power goes to non-computing overhead
- ASHRAE compliance noted - but employee comfort dimension rarely studied
- Supports argument that industry self-reported PUE figures are not representative
