# Growing Waste in HPC-Enabled Academic Practice

**Topic:** Data Centers - Demand Growth and Institutional Drivers
**Source:** Lord, Friday, Jackson, Bird, Preist, Lambert, Kayumbi & Widdicks, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713919

## Insight

This CHI 2025 paper offers a critical sociological analysis of why computing demand keeps growing despite acknowledged environmental impacts. Based on 25 interviews with academic researchers, HPC providers, funders, and commissioners, the study finds that "intersecting socio-cultural and technical dimensions" link research institutions like conferences, funders, and universities in ways that "reinforce and embed, rather than challenge, expectations of growth and waste." The authors explicitly connect this to the current AI moment: "At a time when large scale cloud systems, generative AI and ever larger models are multiplying," they argue for a need to "de-escalate demand for computing."

## Key Data

- 25 interviews conducted with: academic researchers, HPC providers, funders, commissioners
- Institutions identified as reinforcing growth: conferences, funders, universities
- Key mechanisms: technical and cultural drivers that embed rather than challenge growth expectations
- Context: ICT environmental impacts are globally noted but rarely addressed in research innovation processes
- Call to action: "de-escalate demand for computing"
- Scope: HPC specifically, but explicitly connects to generative AI and cloud systems

## Quote

> "At a time when large scale cloud systems, generative AI and ever larger models are multiplying, we argue to de-escalate demand for computing, aiming for more moderate, responsible and meaningful use of computational infrastructures."
