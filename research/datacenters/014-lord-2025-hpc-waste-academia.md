# Interview study argues academic institutions embed expectations of compute growth and waste

**Topic:** Data centres - institutional drivers of compute demand
**Source:** Lord, Friday, Jackson, Bird, Preist, Lambert, Kayumbi & Widdicks (Lancaster University, University of Edinburgh, University of Bristol, STFC, Jisc), 2025
**Type:** peer-reviewed
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713919
**Published:** 2025-04

## Finding

Twenty-five interviews with academic researchers, HPC providers, funders and commissioners are used to argue that conferences, funders and universities reinforce rather than challenge expectations of growth and waste in research computing. The conclusion is an interpretive one drawn from interview accounts. The paper measures no energy, emissions or utilisation, and it makes no estimate of how large academic HPC is relative to commercial data centre demand. Generative AI enters as context for why the authors argue for de-escalating demand, not as a measured object.

## Methodology

Qualitative interview study in the HCI tradition, presented at CHI 2025. Twenty-five semi-structured interviews spanning four stakeholder groups in UK research computing, analysed for the socio-cultural and technical dimensions that link institutional incentives to computing demand. The published abstract and metadata state the sample and the argument; interview recruitment window, coding procedure and analytic approach could not be read from the record obtained.

## Limitations and conflicts

Authors are at UK universities and UK research infrastructure bodies (STFC, Jisc), which are themselves HPC providers and commissioners, so several authors sit inside the institutional system the paper criticises. The paper is gold open access under CC BY at ACM. The full text could not be retrieved: ACM, the Edinburgh, Bristol and Heriot-Watt repositories and the Internet Archive all refused automated requests. Everything recorded here is verified against the published abstract, the Crossref record and the Semantic Scholar record for the version of record. Sample size, sector and country limit generalisation: UK academic HPC is a small and atypical slice of computing demand, and no claim about commercial or AI data centre demand follows from it.

## Relation to existing corpus

Offers an institutional account of why efficiency gains do not reduce total demand, complementing the rebound-effect reasoning reported from primary LCA work in ghg/006. It supplies no figure that confirms or contradicts any measurement in the corpus.
