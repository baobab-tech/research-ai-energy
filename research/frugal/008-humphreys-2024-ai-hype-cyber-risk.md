# AI Hype as a Cyber Security Risk: The Moral Responsibility of Implementing Generative AI in Business

**Topic:** Frugal AI - When NOT to Use AI
**Source:** Humphreys, Koay, Desmond, Mealy, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-024-00443-4

## Insight

Companies rushing to adopt generative AI solutions due to hype and competitive pressure are exposing themselves to significant cybersecurity risks. The race to "buy into AI hype" and not fall behind competitors is creating broad and potentially catastrophic vulnerabilities, including model backdoors that could compromise user data and "poisoned" models producing false results. Over-reliance and over-trust in AI are identified as key ethical concerns that lead to poor business decisions.

## Key Points

- **When NOT to use AI:** When adoption is driven by hype rather than genuine need; when adequate safety measures cannot be implemented; when the rush to deploy bypasses security review
- **Hidden costs of premature AI adoption:** Potential cyber-attacks, data breaches, compromised models, and false outputs that damage business decisions
- **Over-reliance identified as ethical problem:** Favoring fast AI solutions over thoughtful approaches negatively influences business outcomes
- **Ethical framework for AI appropriateness:** Beneficence, non-maleficence, autonomy, justice, and explicability should guide AI adoption decisions
- **Recommendation:** Checklists for ethical AI implementation to minimize risk-based adoption

## Notes
