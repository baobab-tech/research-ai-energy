# TRIPOD+AI Statement: Updated Guidance for Reporting Clinical Prediction Models That Use Regression or Machine Learning Methods

**Topic:** Frugal AI - When NOT to Use AI (Right-Sizing Models)
**Source:** Collins, Moons, Dhiman, Riley, Beam, Van Calster, et al., 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-078378

## Insight

This authoritative BMJ guideline provides harmonized reporting guidance for clinical prediction models "irrespective of whether regression modelling or machine learning methods have been used." The framing explicitly acknowledges that traditional regression methods and AI/ML methods are often interchangeable for prediction tasks - the choice should be based on appropriateness for the data and problem, not hype. The guideline supersedes the 2015 TRIPOD checklist, emphasizing that model selection should be evidence-based rather than technology-driven.

## Key Points

- **Explicit parity between regression and ML:** Guidelines treat traditional regression and machine learning as equivalent options for clinical prediction
- **Right-sizing principle:** Model choice should depend on data characteristics and problem requirements, not AI enthusiasm
- **27-item checklist:** Provides structured evaluation of whether model complexity is justified by the prediction task
- **Reporting transparency:** Requires complete, accurate, transparent reporting regardless of whether "AI" is used
- **Implementation focus:** Emphasizes that model evaluation and implementation matter more than whether it's called "AI"

## Notes

- Published in BMJ with 1,139 citations as of January 2025 - exceptionally influential guideline
- 34 co-authors from leading institutions - represents expert consensus
- Supersedes 2015 TRIPOD checklist - reflects maturation of field
- Key message: The term "AI" doesn't make a prediction model inherently better than regression
- Clinical prediction is high-stakes domain where model appropriateness matters more than novelty
- Applicable principle extends beyond healthcare to any prediction task
