# Synaptic and Neural Behaviours in a Standard Silicon Transistor

**Topic:** Efficient AI / Neuromorphic Hardware
**Source:** Pazos, Zhu, Lanza et al., 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08742-4

## Insight

This Nature paper demonstrates that a single standard CMOS transistor can exhibit neural and synaptic behaviours when biased in a specific (unconventional) manner. By connecting one additional transistor in series, the authors create a "neuro-synaptic random access memory cell" (NS-RAM) that shows adjustable neuro-synaptic response. This is transformative because current neuromorphic systems require at least 18 transistors per neuron and 6 per synapse - this approach reduces that to just 2 transistors total.

## Key Data

- **2 transistors** for combined neuron + synapse function (vs 24+ in current designs)
- **100% yield** - uses mature silicon CMOS process (no exotic materials)
- **Ultra-low device-to-device variability** - critical for reliable neural networks
- **45 citations** as of January 2025 (rapid uptake)
- Published in Nature

## Relevance to Frugal AI

This breakthrough offers a "short-term solution" for implementing efficient ANNs using existing CMOS fabrication:
1. **Massive density improvement** - could enable far larger on-chip neural networks
2. **No new materials required** - uses standard silicon process
3. **Energy efficiency** - simpler circuits mean lower power
4. **Immediate deployability** - no exotic fabrication needed

## Quote

"Simplifying the structure and size of these two building blocks would enable the construction of more sophisticated, larger and more energy-efficient ANNs"

## Critical Note

The authors call this a "short-term solution" suggesting it bridges the gap until more advanced neuromorphic technologies (memristors, photonics) mature. Real-world ANN implementation and energy benchmarks not provided in abstract.
