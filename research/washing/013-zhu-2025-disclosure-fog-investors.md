# The Disclosure Fog: How Institutional Investors Inadvertently Facilitate Greenwashing

**Topic:** Greenwashing - Investor Role
**Source:** Zhu, Fan & Zheng, International Journal of Finance & Economics, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ijfe.3096

## Insight

A study of Chinese listed "heavily polluting" firms from 2012-2021 reveals a paradox in ESG disclosure: while institutional investors encourage firms to publish sustainability reports, they **inadvertently discourage** the use of substantive sustainability-related content within those reports. The paper identifies that corporate ESG disclosure often serves as a "superficial signal rather than something of substance" - creating a "disclosure fog" that obscures rather than illuminates environmental performance.

## Key Mechanisms

The study identifies three pathways through which institutional investors influence greenwashing:
1. **Corporate governance** - Investors focus on governance metrics that may not capture environmental authenticity
2. **Information transparency** - More disclosure requirements paradoxically create more opportunities for selective reporting
3. **Corporate operations** - Pressure for short-term financial performance conflicts with long-term environmental investment

## Critical Finding

**Pressure-sensitive investors** (those with business relationships with the firm) are MORE likely to facilitate greenwashing than pressure-insensitive or pressure-uncertain investors. This suggests conflicts of interest compromise investor oversight of environmental claims.

## Notes

- Published in International Journal of Finance & Economics (10 citations)
- Authors from Shenzhen University
- Study period: 2012-2021 Chinese listed heavily polluting firms
- Post-2016 "Guidance on Building Green Financial System" implementation: the positive relationship between institutional investors and greenwashing rapidly declined, suggesting regulation works
- Implications: Tech company ESG claims need scrutiny of WHO is verifying them - investors with business relationships may not be reliable watchdogs
