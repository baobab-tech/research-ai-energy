# Under stricter Scope 2 rules, the share of companies behind their climate targets rises from 28% to 50%

**Topic:** Greenwashing evidence — REC additionality and deliverability; quantified effect on reported target progress
**Source:** Anders Bjørn, Jens Friis Lund, Matthew Brander, Environmental Research Letters 20(2)
**Type:** peer-reviewed
**URL:** https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/ada45a
**Published:** 2025-01-10

## Finding

Bjørn, Lund and Brander recomputed the market-based Scope 2 emissions of 206 companies after invalidating the renewable energy certificates that would fail proposed additionality and deliverability criteria. The share of sampled companies behind their 2022 climate target rises from 28% to 50% under the combined restrictions. This is the quantified basis for the claim that market-based Scope 2 accounting overstates corporate decarbonisation, and it is the literature the hyperscalers' April 2026 opposition letter argues against.

## Key Data

| Metric | Value | Boundary / method |
|--------|-------|-------------------|
| Companies analysed | 206 | filtered from 10,832 CDP 2023 disclosers |
| Filter | complete disclosure of REC purchases and facility characteristics | excludes incomplete disclosers |
| Share behind target, current rules (2022) | 28% | as reported |
| Share behind target, all three restrictions applied | 50% | recomputed |
| Restriction 1 | geographical proximity: RECs valid only from the country of consumption | deliverability |
| Restriction 2 | facility age: RECs valid only from facilities commissioned within 36 months of purchase | additionality proxy |
| Restriction 3 | contractual form: RECs valid only when sourced through PPAs | additionality proxy |

## Methodology

Attributional recomputation, not a consequential emissions model. For each company the authors determined what fraction of purchased RECs would remain valid under each proposed criterion and each combination, then adjusted reported market-based Scope 2 accordingly and re-evaluated progress against that company's own stated target. The study does not estimate physical grid emissions; it measures how much of reported target progress depends on certificates that would fail the proposed tests. Sample is restricted to CDP disclosers with complete REC and facility data, so it is skewed toward larger and more transparent reporters.

## Limitations and conflicts

No funding information was disclosed in the article. The 36-month facility-age threshold and the PPA-only restriction are proxies for additionality rather than measurements of it; a REC from an older facility can still be additional in a market where certificate revenue determines continued operation, and a new PPA is not automatically additional. The sample's bias toward complete disclosers likely understates the effect across the broader universe of reporters. The paper does not analyse hyperscalers specifically, which is why it belongs alongside the company-level files here rather than replacing them. Matthew Brander has published extensively arguing against the market-based method, so the framing is not neutral; the arithmetic, however, is reproducible from CDP data.
