# Google excludes 4.25 million tCO2e of Scope 3 from the boundary it measures its net-zero target against

**Topic:** Greenwashing evidence — Scope 3 boundary drawing; supply-chain emissions of AI hardware
**Source:** Google/Alphabet, 2026 Environmental Report (calendar year 2025)
**Type:** corporate disclosure
**URL:** https://sustainability.google/files/google-2026-environmental-report.pdf
**Published:** 2026-06-30

## Finding

Google reports two total footprints. Its GHG Protocol-aligned 2025 emissions are 18,847,100 tCO2e. The figure it uses to track its 2030 net-zero ambition — "ambition-based emissions" — is 14,473,100 tCO2e, and Google names this "the primary metric for tracking progress toward our net zero climate ambition." The 4.37 Mt difference comes from 4,251,000 tCO2e of Scope 3 "ambition boundary exclusions" plus 123,000 tCO2e of "market instrument reductions beyond what GHGP currently recognizes." 23% of Google's disclosed inventory therefore sits outside the target it reports against. Scope 3 is 85% of the GHGP-aligned total, and the largest excluded block is Category 2 capital goods plus Category 11 use of sold products — the categories containing chip fabrication, server manufacture and data centre construction.

## Methodology

Both series appear in the same environmental metrics data table, so the exclusions are disclosed rather than hidden; what is at issue is which figure Google reports progress against. Google states its ambition-based metric "incorporates adjustments to scope 3 — specifically ambition-based boundary exclusions and market instrument reductions beyond what GHGP currently recognizes." 2025 metrics were subject to third-party limited assurance. Alphabet's near-term target was validated by SBTi in 2025 under Corporate Near-Term Criteria v5.2 using the cross-sector absolute reduction method; the report does not state whether SBTi validated the ambition-based boundary or the GHGP-aligned one.

Category 1 exclusions are the largest single block at 2,967,000 tCO2e, removing 74% of that category. Google does not itemise in the data table which suppliers or activities fall inside each exclusion.

## Limitations and conflicts

Self-reported and self-defined. The exclusions are Google's own boundary choices, not a GHGP convention, and they have grown 81% since 2019 in absolute terms while the GHGP-aligned inventory grew 82% — so the excluded share has stayed roughly constant rather than widening. Google's stated rationale is prioritising categories where it has influence; it notes Scope 1 is under 1% of its footprint and that it concentrates on Scopes 2 and 3 where reduction potential is larger. The report's "without our decarbonization interventions our footprint would have been five times larger" claim is a counterfactual with no stated independent verification.
