# Mapping the Greenwashing Research Landscape: Multi-Level Theoretical Framework

**Topic:** Greenwashing - Theoretical Framework
**Source:** Forliano et al., Review of Managerial Science, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11846-025-00856-3

## Insight

A systematic review of 97 peer-reviewed greenwashing articles identifies four thematic clusters dominating the field: (1) symbolic management and CSR communication, (2) environmental regulations and institutional complexity, (3) performance and sustainable practices, and (4) marketing, perception, and trust. Critically, the analysis reveals that **macro-level theories dominate** - legitimacy theory, institutional theory, and stakeholder theory are most frequently used to explain greenwashing. This suggests greenwashing is best understood as a systemic feature of how firms respond to institutional pressures, not merely as individual corporate misbehavior.

## Key Finding

The paper develops a multi-level theoretical framework showing how greenwashing operates across:
- **Macro level:** Institutional pressures and regulatory gaps create incentives for symbolic rather than substantive environmental action
- **Meso level:** Industry norms and competitive dynamics influence what firms feel they must claim
- **Micro level:** Individual managerial decisions and organizational routines execute greenwashing tactics

## Implications for AI/Tech Claims

This framework suggests that tech company environmental claims (carbon neutrality, water positivity, renewable energy) should be analyzed through institutional lens - asking what **pressures** drive claims rather than just fact-checking individual statements. The dominance of legitimacy theory implies companies greenwash primarily to maintain social license, especially when actual performance lags stakeholder expectations.
