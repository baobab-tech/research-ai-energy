# Carbon Farming and Overestimated Negative Emissions in EU Policy

**Topic:** Greenwashing/Carbon Removal Claims
**Source:** Gunther et al., Environmental Sciences Europe, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s12302-024-00892-y

## Insight

The study critically examines the EU Carbon Removal Certification proposal and finds that carbon farming schemes systematically overestimate their negative emissions potential. The authors argue that including land-use carbon credits in emissions trading creates risks of "greenwashing" at the policy level, as carbon storage in agricultural soils and forests is impermanent, difficult to verify, and often overstated, allowing emitters to claim offsets without equivalent climate benefit.

## Key Claims vs Reality

- **Claim:** Carbon farming and land-use credits provide equivalent climate benefit to emissions reductions
- **Reality:** Carbon stored in soils and biomass is impermanent and reversible (through fire, disease, land-use change), creating fundamental asymmetry with fossil fuel emissions avoided

- **Claim:** Certification frameworks can accurately quantify and verify carbon removal
- **Reality:** The study finds that measurement uncertainties, baseline issues, and additionality problems lead to systematic overestimation of negative emissions
