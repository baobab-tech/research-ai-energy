# Detecting Greenwashing Through Textual Analysis: Impression Management Gaps

**Topic:** Greenwashing - Detection Methods
**Source:** Sneideriene & Legenzova, Sustainability, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/su17188342

## Insight

A novel methodology for detecting greenwashing by measuring the **"impression management gap"** between sustainability reports and management (financial) reports from the same company. Using textual analysis with Linguistic Inquiry and Word Count (LIWC) software on 192 reports from 24 companies (2020-2023), the study found significant gaps in tone, clout, and analytical thinking dimensions - indicating that companies use different impression management techniques in sustainability vs. financial reporting. Critically, **despite increasing regulatory pressure during the study period, the calculated gaps did not decrease** - suggesting greenwashing persists even under scrutiny.

## Key Finding

Measurable greenwashing indicators across four dimensions:
- **Tone:** Sustainability reports significantly more positive than financial reports
- **Clout:** Sustainability reports use more authoritative/confident language
- **Analytical thinking:** Differences in logical vs. emotional framing
- **Authenticity:** Gaps in perceived genuineness of communication

**Sector variation:** Services and Manufacturing sectors showed highest likelihood of greenwashing; Utilities sector showed lower likelihood.

## Methodological Value

This approach provides a **replicable quantitative method** for detecting greenwashing that could be applied to tech company sustainability reports:
1. Compare sustainability report language to financial report language
2. Flag significant tone/clout/authenticity gaps as greenwashing indicators
3. Track changes over time to assess if regulation is effective

## Notes

- Published in Sustainability (1 citation - very recent publication Sept 2025)
- Authors from Vytautas Magnus University, Lithuania
- Sample: 192 reports from 24 companies, 2020-2023
- Key finding: Increased regulatory pressure did NOT reduce impression management gaps
- Limitation: Sample limited to 24 companies; applicability to AI/tech sector not tested
- Application potential: Could be applied to Google, Microsoft, Meta sustainability reports vs. financial disclosures
