# Meta reports Scope 2 emissions of 1,358 tCO2e against 5,967,348 tCO2e of grid emissions — a 4,394-fold gap

**Topic:** Greenwashing evidence — market-based Scope 2; "100% renewable" claims
**Source:** Meta Platforms, 2025 Environmental Data Index (calendar year 2024)
**Type:** corporate disclosure
**URL:** https://sustainability.atmeta.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Meta_2025-Environmental-Data-Index.pdf
**Published:** 2025-10

## Finding

Meta's reported market-based Scope 2 emissions for 2024 are 1,358 tCO2e. Its location-based Scope 2 for the same facilities and the same year is 5,967,348 tCO2e. The market-based figure is 0.023% of the location-based one. Across 2020-2024 Meta's location-based Scope 2 rose 120% (2,718,000 → 5,967,348 tCO2e) while its market-based Scope 2 fell 85% (9,000 → 1,358 tCO2e). This is the widest divergence between the two GHGP methods among the four large hyperscalers, and it is what allows Meta to report total operational emissions (Scope 1 + 2 market-based) of 48,826 tCO2e against a total location-based footprint of 15,627,509 tCO2e.

## Methodology

Meta publishes both methods side by side in a dedicated "Market-Based vs. Location-Based" table, which is more granular disclosure than Amazon provides. Market-based emissions apply energy attribute certificates and PPAs matched annually to consumption; Meta states it has matched 100% of its electricity use with clean and renewable energy since 2020 and supports roughly 29 GW of wind and solar. Location-based uses grid-average factors for the regions where consumption occurs. Meta notes that prior to fiscal 2024 its Scope 3 figures combined GHGP and market-based adjustments.

The per-MWh intensity metric Meta reports uses the market-based numerator, so it falls as certificate coverage rises independently of grid carbon intensity.

## Limitations and conflicts

Self-reported. Meta's position is that its long-term PPAs bring new wind and solar onto grids that would not otherwise have them, and its disclosure of the location-based series is more complete than several peers'. The 4,394x ratio is not itself evidence of deception — it is the arithmetic consequence of annual volumetric matching — but it means Meta's headline operational emissions number conveys nothing about the emissions of the electricity its data centres physically consumed. Meta's net-zero-by-2030 and 42%-reduction-by-2031 targets are defined against Scope 1 and 2 market-based figures, so the target can be met without the location-based series falling.
