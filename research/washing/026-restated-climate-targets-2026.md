# Microsoft stopped publishing its carbon-neutral and renewable-percentage metrics; Google reframed net-zero 2030 as an "intentionally aspirational" moonshot

**Topic:** Greenwashing evidence — targets quietly restated, redefined or retired in 2025-2026
**Source:** Microsoft 2026 Environmental Data Fact Sheet; Google 2026 Environmental Report
**Type:** corporate disclosure
**URL:** https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/2026-Microsoft-Environmental-Data-Fact-Sheet-PDF.pdf
**Published:** 2026

## Finding

Both companies changed what they report against without changing the headline pledge. Microsoft states it has "stopped tracking a year-over-year separate carbon neutral metric and percentage of renewable electricity metric," having previously published both annually, and concedes the change "may temporarily move us out of a carbon-neutral position." Its "percentage of direct renewable electricity" series ends at 100% for FY2025 and is discontinued. Google, whose 2021 commitment was to "reach net-zero emissions across all of our operations and value chain by 2030," now describes that commitment and its 24/7 carbon-free energy goal as "moonshots" that were "intentionally aspirational" and picked when "we didn't yet have the technology or the systems to reach" them, writing that "our climate moonshot is getting harder." Neither company withdrew or reset a target date. Both retained the 2030 date while changing the measurement basis or the epistemic status of the claim.

## Methodology

Both statements are drawn from the companies' own 2026 disclosures. Microsoft's discontinuations are stated in the methodology notes of the Environmental Data Fact Sheet, not in the report's headline sections. Google's reframing appears in the executive summary and the Climate ambitions section; the 2030 net-zero target statement itself is unchanged in wording. Microsoft's FY runs July-June; Google reports calendar years.

Microsoft's stated rationale is a shift toward "more long-term, higher impact investments across carbon reduction, carbon removal, and carbon-free energy procurement," with its renewable programme focused on long-term PPAs, and it frames the change as "high-integrity climate action" — a defensible position, since the metrics it retired were the ones flattered by the certificates it stopped buying. Google's stated rationale is that progress "is being shaped by rapid shifts in technology and the slow evolution of global power grids" and that it has a responsibility "to report on this journey with clarity."

## Limitations and conflicts

Self-reported. Neither change is concealed: both appear in the companies' published documents, and Microsoft's is arguably a move toward stricter accounting rather than away from it. What is documented here is the pattern, not deception — a target date held constant while the metric that measures it is retired or relabelled as aspirational, in the same reporting cycle in which the underlying emissions rose 25% (Microsoft, FY2025) and 18% (Google, 2025). Whether "moonshot" was Google's framing in 2021 cannot be established from the 2026 report alone; the report states "as we've mentioned in previous reports" regarding the non-linear path.
