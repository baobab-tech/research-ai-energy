# Limited Accountability for Corporate Emissions Target Outcomes

**Topic:** Greenwashing - Reporting Gaps
**Source:** Jiang et al., Nature Climate Change, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41558-024-02236-3

## Insight

A study of 1,041 corporate emissions targets ending in 2020 reveals a stunning accountability vacuum: 88 firms (9%) failed their targets and 320 (31%) simply made their targets "disappear" with no follow-up disclosure. Only THREE of the 88 failed targets received any media coverage. After failure, researchers found no significant market reaction, no changes in media sentiment, no impact on environmental scores, and no increase in shareholder proposals - while initial target ANNOUNCEMENTS were rewarded with improved scores and positive coverage.

## Key Gaps Identified

- **Announcement vs. outcome asymmetry:** Companies receive credit for announcing targets but face no consequences for failing them - creating incentive to announce ambitious targets without delivery
- **Disappearing targets:** 31% of targets simply vanish from corporate reporting with no explanation or accountability
- **Media blindness:** Only 3% of failed targets received any media coverage, enabling silent failure
- **Score manipulation:** Environmental ratings improve upon announcement but don't decrease upon failure, making ESG scores a lagging indicator at best
- **Implications for 2030/2050:** The authors explicitly warn these findings "raise concerns for accountability of emissions targets ending in 2030 and 2050"

## Notes

- Published in Nature Climate Change (23 citations, published Dec 2024)
- Authors from University of Pittsburgh and Harvard Business School
- Methodology: Analysis of 1,041 firm-year emissions targets with 2020 end dates
- Critical finding: Current ESG/sustainability reporting incentivizes target-setting theater over actual emissions reduction
