# From CSR to ESG to Greenwashing: Comprehensive Review with Corporate Case Studies

**Topic:** Greenwashing - Case Studies
**Source:** Zervoudi, Moschos & Christopoulos, Sustainability, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/su17052222

## Insight

A comprehensive literature review traces the evolution from Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) to Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) criteria, documenting how this evolution has created new opportunities for greenwashing. The paper provides **specific corporate case studies** of greenwashing including Volkswagen (emissions cheating), Zara (fast fashion sustainability claims), Coca-Cola (plastic pollution), and BP ("Beyond Petroleum" rebranding). The analysis highlights that greenwashing can undermine trust not just in individual companies but in entire industries and the progress toward sustainability.

## Key Finding

The paper documents the systemic nature of greenwashing by examining:
- **Historical context:** How environmental marketing has evolved since 1980s
- **Regulatory gaps:** Current regulations insufficient to prevent misleading claims
- **Market distortions:** Greenwashing creates unfair advantage over genuinely sustainable competitors
- **Environmental harm:** Delayed climate action due to false sense of progress

## Case Study Relevance

The Volkswagen case (installing "defeat devices" in 11 million vehicles to cheat emissions tests) demonstrates how sophisticated environmental fraud can be:
- Public claims of "clean diesel" technology
- Multi-year deception across regulatory jurisdictions
- Tens of billions in eventual penalties

This pattern of sophisticated environmental claims masking opposite reality is directly relevant to evaluating tech company claims about carbon neutrality and renewable energy.
