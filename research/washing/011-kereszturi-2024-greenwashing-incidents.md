# Incident-Based Greenwashing Detection: What ESG Scores Miss

**Topic:** Greenwashing - Reporting Gaps
**Source:** Kereszturi et al., Corporate Social Responsibility and Environmental Management, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1002/csr.2945

## Insight

A novel incident-based methodology reveals that standard ESG scoring systematically misses greenwashing behavior. The study found that about 7% of 1,218 large and mid-cap companies in the MSCI World Index engaged in greenwashing at least once between 2008-2020, defined as improving their environmental ESG subscore while simultaneously being responsible for severe environmental damage. This exposes a fundamental flaw: ESG scores can IMPROVE even as actual environmental harm occurs.

## Key Gaps Identified

- **ESG score gaming:** Companies can increase environmental ESG subscores while causing severe environmental damage - scores measure disclosure/policy, not outcomes
- **Sector concentration:** Greenwashing is highest in energy, utilities, and materials industries - the sectors with largest environmental footprints
- **Self-reporting failure:** Traditional ESG assessment relies on company self-reporting which misses actual environmental incidents
- **Monitoring gap:** Both internal (board independence) and external (investor attention) monitoring reduce greenwashing, but many companies lack effective oversight
- **Geographic variation:** Companies in countries with higher environmental awareness are less likely to greenwash, suggesting disclosure norms matter

## Notes

- Published in Corporate Social Responsibility and Environmental Management (19 citations)
- Authors from Corvinus University of Budapest
- Methodology: Cross-country analysis of MSCI World Index constituents (23 developed markets)
- Key innovation: Uses actual environmental incidents rather than self-reported ESG data
- Finding underscores need for third-party verification of environmental claims
