# Accounting Reporting Complexity Enables Selective ESG Disclosure

**Topic:** Greenwashing - Reporting Mechanisms
**Source:** Al Amosh, Corporate Social Responsibility and Environmental Management, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1002/csr.70000

## Insight

A study of 5,146 firm-year observations from U.S. companies (2012-2021) finds that **complex accounting reporting structures have a significant negative impact on ESG disclosure quality**, particularly for environmental disclosures. The paper applies agency theory and information manipulation theory (IMT) to explain how complexity creates conditions conducive to selective presentation of ESG information - managers use complexity to "shape disclosures in ways that amplify favorable outcomes while obscuring less impressive results."

## Key Finding

- Complex financial reporting frameworks enable managers to exercise **selective discretion** in what ESG data to disclose
- **Environmental disclosures most affected** - complexity has strongest negative impact on environmental reporting
- Social and governance disclosures show weaker (only marginally significant) negative effects
- Information asymmetry increases with complexity, raising stakeholder monitoring costs

## Theoretical Mechanism

From **Agency Theory:** Managers already possess deeper knowledge of firm operations; complex accounting systems further expand their discretion in deciding what to highlight or suppress.

From **Information Manipulation Theory:** Complex reporting enables managers to shape disclosures to amplify favorable outcomes while obscuring unfavorable results - stakeholders cannot verify every data point.

## Implications for Tech Company Claims

Tech companies have notoriously complex corporate structures (subsidiaries, holding companies, offshore entities) and reporting frameworks. This research suggests such complexity may **systematically enable** selective environmental disclosure - including claims about carbon neutrality, renewable energy, and water consumption that cannot easily be verified by outsiders.

## Notes

- Published in Corporate Social Responsibility and Environmental Management (12 citations)
- Author from University of South Africa
- Methodology: Panel regression on 5,146 U.S. firm-year observations 2012-2021
- Key implication: Standardized ESG reporting frameworks are essential to reduce manipulation opportunities
- Policy recommendation: Regulators should consider reporting complexity when designing sustainability disclosure rules
