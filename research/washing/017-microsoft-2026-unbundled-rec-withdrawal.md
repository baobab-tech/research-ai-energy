# Dropping unbundled RECs multiplied Microsoft's reported Scope 2 emissions tenfold in one year

**Topic:** Greenwashing evidence — market-based Scope 2 accounting; unbundled REC additionality
**Source:** Microsoft Corporation, 2026 Environmental Data Fact Sheet (FY2025)
**Type:** corporate disclosure
**URL:** https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/2026-Microsoft-Environmental-Data-Fact-Sheet-PDF.pdf
**Published:** 2026

## Finding

Microsoft stopped buying "spot" unbundled energy attribute certificates in February 2025. Its reported market-based Scope 2 emissions rose from 259,090 tCO2e in FY2024 to 2,707,428 tCO2e in FY2025 — a 10.4x increase produced by an accounting change, not by a change in electricity sourcing. Microsoft states in the same document that "our carbon neutrality claims were supported in part by spot purchases of unbundled EACs and carbon removal credits from existing projects," and that discontinuing them "may temporarily move us out of a carbon-neutral position." This is a hyperscaler's own confirmation that a prior carbon-neutral claim rested on certificates it now describes as non-additional.

## Methodology

Microsoft defines spot EACs as unbundled EACs that are (1) not utility-contracted and (2) acquired under short-term agreements with delivery of the contracted volume within two years of contract execution. Market-based Scope 2 retains on-site generation, PPAs, green power products and other long-term contracts for environmental attributes. Location-based Scope 2 uses grid-average factors (eGRID for the US, European residual mixes, IEA elsewhere). FY runs July–June, so FY2025 ends 30 June 2025 and captures roughly five months after the February 2025 policy change.

The 100%-direct-renewable metric and the 12.03 Mt location-based figure are computed from the same electricity volume in the same document. Microsoft states it has "stopped tracking a year-over-year separate carbon neutral metric and percentage of renewable electricity metric" going forward.

## Limitations and conflicts

Self-reported and self-defined. Microsoft's FY2025 figures were subject to third-party assurance, but the split between "spot" and "long-term" certificates is Microsoft's own classification and the two-year delivery threshold is its own cutoff; contracts just outside it remain in the market-based figure. The location-based series is the closer proxy for physical grid impact, and it rose every year through the transition. The company frames the change as "high-integrity climate action" and reports it would have emitted over 34 Mt CO2e in FY2025 absent its interventions — a counterfactual with no stated verification.
