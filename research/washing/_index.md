# Greenwashing/Bluewashing Index

Two tiers. Tier 1 is AI-specific: documented discrepancy between a named company's environmental claim and
its own underlying data (1a), plus academic work on AI sustainability claims (1b). Tier 2 is the
general greenwashing literature, retained as framing; none of it concerns technology companies or AI.

## Tier 1a — Hyperscaler disclosures (primary documents)

Documented, quantified discrepancies between hyperscaler environmental claims and their own underlying data.

| # | Source | Insight |
|---|--------|---------|
| 017 | [Microsoft, 2026](017-microsoft-2026-unbundled-rec-withdrawal.md) | Dropping unbundled spot EACs raised reported market-based Scope 2 from 259,090 to 2,707,428 tCO2e (10.4x); Microsoft states prior carbon-neutral claims rested on those certificates |
| 018 | [Google, 2026](018-google-2026-market-vs-location-based-gap.md) | 2025 Scope 2: 15,148,700 tCO2e location-based vs 2,815,000 market-based; the headline "58 Mt avoided" is defined in the endnotes as that gap, and is unverified |
| 019 | [Google, 2026](019-google-2026-ambition-boundary-exclusions.md) | 4.37 Mt CO2e (23% of the disclosed inventory), mostly Scope 3 capital goods and purchased goods, sits outside the boundary Google tracks its net-zero target against |
| 020 | [Meta, 2025](020-meta-2025-scope2-4394x-gap.md) | Scope 2 reported at 1,358 tCO2e market-based against 5,967,348 tCO2e location-based — a 4,394x gap, the widest among hyperscalers |
| 021 | [WattTime, 2025](021-watttime-2025-meta-carbon-matching.md) | Consequential accounting puts Meta's 2023 data centre net emissions at 770,000 t vs 733 t reported; Meta's procurement did avoid ~7.35 Mt of 8.12 Mt induced |
| 022 | [Amazon, 2026](022-amazon-2026-market-based-only-no-location-disclosure.md) | Publishes no location-based Scope 2 at all; claims 100% renewable matching while market-based Scope 2 rose 34% to 3.74 MMTCO2e; applies EACs against Scope 1 |
| 023 | [Google & Amazon, 2026](023-water-positive-pledge-accounting-2026.md) | "Water positive" reported at 78% and 75% of consumption; replenishment pooled across global project portfolios rather than matched to depleted basins |
| 024 | [InfluenceMap, 2026](024-influencemap-2026-ghgp-scope2-lobbying.md) | Google publicly backed stricter GHGP Scope 2 rules; Amazon and Apple signed a 66-party letter opposing mandatory hourly matching; Microsoft and Meta filed nothing public |
| 025 | [Bjørn, Lund & Brander, 2025](025-bjorn-2025-stricter-scope2-rules.md) | Invalidating non-additional RECs raises the share of companies behind their climate target from 28% to 50% (n=206) |
| 026 | [Microsoft & Google, 2026](026-restated-climate-targets-2026.md) | Microsoft retired its carbon-neutral and renewable-percentage metrics; Google reframed net-zero 2030 as an "intentionally aspirational" moonshot — both 2030 dates unchanged |

## Tier 2 — General greenwashing literature (background, not AI-specific)

These sources establish mechanisms — institutional pressure, disclosure gaming, offset quality, detection
methods — that the Tier 1 evidence instantiates. They make no claim about AI or data centres.
