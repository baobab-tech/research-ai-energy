# AI Emits 130-2900x Less CO2 Than Humans for Equivalent Tasks

**Topic:** Narrative - Net Impact Analysis
**Source:** Tomlinson, Black, Patterson & Torrance, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-54271-x

## Insight

This Nature Scientific Reports paper presents a direct comparison of carbon emissions between AI systems (ChatGPT, BLOOM, DALL-E2, Midjourney) and humans performing equivalent writing and illustration tasks. AI emits 130-1500x less CO2e per page of text than human writers, and 310-2900x less CO2e per image than human illustrators. CRITICALLY, the authors explicitly acknowledge their analysis "does not account for social impacts such as professional displacement, legality, and rebound effects." They state AI "is not a substitute for all human tasks" but argue AI "holds the potential to carry out several major activities at much lower emission levels than can humans."

## Benefits vs Costs

| Benefits | Costs |
|----------|-------|
| Per-task emissions dramatically lower (130-2900x) | Rebound effects explicitly NOT included |
| ChatGPT and BLOOM lower than DALL-E2, Midjourney | Professional displacement impacts ignored |
| Text generation more efficient than image generation | AI cannot substitute all human creative work |
| Could reduce emissions for specific use cases | Scale effects not considered (what if 100x more content?) |

## Net Assessment

- **Is AI net positive or negative for environment?** Per-task comparison shows AI as far more efficient. But authors acknowledge this is a narrow framing that ignores rebound effects - the tendency for efficiency gains to increase total consumption.
- **What's missing from the analysis?** (1) Rebound effects - if AI makes content creation cheap, will humans create vastly more content? (2) Lifecycle emissions of AI hardware (GPU manufacturing, data centers) (3) Water consumption (4) What happens to displaced workers' consumption patterns? (5) Macro-level effects vs task-level efficiency

## Critical Notes

- Paper widely cited as proof AI is "green"
- But authors themselves caveat heavily about limitations
- Key quote: "Emissions analyses do not account for social impacts such as professional displacement, legality, and rebound effects"
- The 130-2900x figure assumes substitution without expansion - a questionable assumption
- Human emissions calculation includes commuting, office space, etc. - broader system boundary than AI calculation
- 43 citations as of 2025
