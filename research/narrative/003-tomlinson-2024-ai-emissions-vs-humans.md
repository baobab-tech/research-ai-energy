# AI vs Human Emissions: A Contested Comparison

**Topic:** Narrative - AI for Climate Claims
**Source:** Tomlinson, Black, Patterson, Torrance, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-54271-x

## Insight

This study claims AI systems emit 130-1500x less CO2e per page of text generated compared to human writers, and 310-2900x less CO2e per image than human illustrators. While presented as evidence AI could reduce emissions, the authors themselves acknowledge critical limitations: the analysis does not account for social impacts (professional displacement, legality), rebound effects, or the fact that AI is not a substitute for all human tasks. This paper exemplifies how narrow emissions comparisons can create misleading narratives about AI as a climate solution.

## Claims vs Evidence

- **Claim:** AI writing/illustration has lower carbon footprint than humans
- **Evidence:** Per-task comparison shows AI produces less CO2e per output unit

- **Claim:** AI could carry out activities at "much lower emission levels than humans"
- **Evidence:** Authors explicitly acknowledge they do NOT account for rebound effects, where efficiency gains lead to increased total consumption

## Notes

- Published in Nature Scientific Reports - high-profile venue
- 43 citations - widely shared
- Critical methodological gap: excludes rebound effects (Jevons paradox)
- Does not account for: professional displacement impacts, legal questions, AI not being substitute for all tasks
- Example of how true but incomplete comparisons support AI-for-climate narratives
- The paper's own caveats are often lost when findings are cited in industry messaging
