# Does AI Enhance Green Economy Efficiency? Evidence from China's Marine Fisheries

**Topic:** Narrative - AI-for-Sustainability Claims (Sectoral Evidence)
**Source:** Wang, Sun & Li, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-024-04319-0
**Journal:** Humanities and Social Sciences Communications (Nature Portfolio)
**Citations:** 47

## Insight

This 2025 study provides empirical evidence for AI's contribution to green economy efficiency in China's marine fisheries sector (2009-2020). Using panel data from 11 coastal provinces, the authors find AI "significantly enhances" green economic efficiency when combined with green finance, trade openness, and R&D investment. However, the study reveals crucial contextual dependencies: AI's impact varies across different intervals of these moderating factors, and regional disparities are substantial. This represents the emerging genre of sectoral studies that provide nuanced evidence for "AI for sustainability" claims.

## Key Findings

1. **AI improves green economy efficiency** - But only under specific conditions
2. **Context matters critically**:
   - Green finance acts as crucial moderating variable
   - Trade openness accelerates AI's positive impact
   - R&D investment amplifies benefits
3. **Regional disparities are substantial** - Not uniform benefit across all provinces
4. **Threshold effects exist** - AI impact varies across different intervals of moderating variables

## Net Impact Assessment

| Pro | Con |
|-----|-----|
| Empirical evidence AI can improve environmental efficiency | Specific to one sector in one country |
| Shows conditions under which AI works | Results depend on complementary policies |
| Identifies moderating factors | Doesn't measure AI's own environmental footprint |
| 11-year panel data adds robustness | Green finance availability varies globally |

## Critical Gaps

1. **No measurement of AI's own footprint**: The study measures AI's benefits without subtracting AI's own water/energy consumption
2. **Correlation vs causation**: Wealthy coastal provinces may adopt both AI and green practices independently
3. **Moderator dependency**: Without green finance and R&D investment, AI may not deliver benefits
4. **Generalizability**: China's marine fisheries may not represent other sectors or countries

## Implication for AI-Climate Narrative

This paper supports "AI for sustainability" claims but with heavy qualifications. AI is not automatically beneficial - it requires complementary investments and policies. The substantial regional disparities suggest AI may exacerbate rather than reduce environmental inequities without proper governance.
