# IEA's 1 400 Mt avoided-emissions figure for AI is an exploratory case with no adoption pathway behind it

**Topic:** narrative — the industry/agency avoided-emissions claim and what it assumes
**Source:** International Energy Agency, 2025 — *Energy and AI*, chapter "AI and climate change"
**Type:** agency report
**URL:** https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/ai-and-climate-change
**Published:** 2025-04

## Finding

The most-cited "AI will help the climate" number — 1 400 Mt CO2 of emissions reductions in 2035 — is the output of an IEA scenario the IEA itself calls exploratory and says has no momentum behind it. The figure comes from a "Widespread Adoption Case" that assumes existing AI applications in end-use sectors are deployed broadly; the report publishes no adoption rate, no sectoral breakdown in text, and no counterfactual definition. Data centre emissions are not netted off: the IEA presents the two quantities side by side ("three times larger than the total data centre emissions in the Lift-off Case"), which is a comparison, not a net calculation. The IEA states plainly that the aggregate impact "could be marginal if the necessary enabling conditions are not created" and that the reductions "could be negated by rebound effects."

| Metric | Value | Boundary / method |

## Methodology

Scenario modelling, not measurement. Five mechanisms are named — methane leak detection in oil and gas, efficiency gains at fossil-fuel power plants, industrial process optimisation, transport routing, and building management systems — each with an illustrative percentage. The text gives no weighting, no adoption curve, and no statement of the baseline against which reductions are counted. The 1 400 Mt appears only as a chart caption ("Direct and indirect emissions reductions in end-use sectors, Widespread Adoption Case, 2035") with no numeric breakdown in the prose. The Widespread Adoption Case is not one of the report's main demand cases (Base, Lift-Off, High Efficiency, Headwinds); it is an adjunct exploratory case, labelled in the report's own chart title as "an exploratory analysis of AI impacts on emissions."

Two of the five named mechanisms increase fossil-fuel system productivity rather than displacing it: methane leak detection at oil and gas operations, and efficiency improvements at gas-fired power plants. Neither is modelled for any production or lifetime effect on the underlying asset.

## Limitations and conflicts

The IEA is an intergovernmental body whose membership is largely OECD energy ministries; the report was produced with input from industry, and the data centre capacity data underlying adjacent charts is licensed from Omdia, a commercial market-intelligence firm. The avoided-emissions figure is the weakest-specified quantity in the chapter: no adoption assumption, no counterfactual, no uncertainty range, no sector split in text. The report does not net the 1 400 Mt against AI's own footprint, and does not quantify the rebound effect it names (modal shift from public transport to autonomous cars). Citation of this number without the "no momentum" and "could be marginal" qualifiers misrepresents the source.
