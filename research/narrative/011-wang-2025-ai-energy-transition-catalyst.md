# The most-cited peer-reviewed statement that AI is a "transformative catalyst" for the energy transition reports no synthesis statistic, no adoption rate and no accounting of AI's own load

**Topic:** narrative — what the academic case for AI as a decarbonisation tool actually consists of
**Source:** Qiang Wang, Yuanfan Li, Rongrong Li, School of Economics and Management, China University of Petroleum (East China), 2025
**Type:** peer-reviewed (narrative review)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esr.2024.101600
**Published:** 2025-01

## Finding

This review surveys AI applications across renewable deployment, energy efficiency, grid stability and smart-grid integration and concludes that AI "is not just a tool but a transformative catalyst, reshaping global energy systems into equitable, resilient, and sustainable frameworks, essential for achieving a net-zero future." The conclusion is reached by synthesising published research and case studies, not by pooling effect sizes: the review reports no quantity of energy saved or emissions avoided, no adoption rate for any of the applications it surveys, no counterfactual against which a saving would be measured, and no netting of the energy AI itself consumes. The limits it does state are implementation frictions: data quality and accessibility, system interoperability, scalability, and privacy and ethics. At 227 citations it is the most-cited academic formulation of the catalyst claim for energy systems specifically, which makes it the claim the primary work elsewhere in this folder is tested against.

## Methodology

A narrative review synthesising recent research and practical case studies across energy-transition domains, with strategic recommendations for research, development and policy. The published abstract states no search string, database set, record counts, screening protocol, inclusion criteria or coding scheme, and the article is not presented as a systematic review. The evidentiary basis for the catalyst conclusion is therefore the authors' reading of a corpus whose composition is not stated. Nothing in the paper is a measurement.

## Limitations and conflicts

Funded by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (71874203 and 72104246) and the Department of Education of Shandong Province (2023RW015). All three authors are at the China University of Petroleum (East China); the same lead and senior author pair published a province-level panel on AI and fisheries green-economy efficiency in the same period, indicating a review-and-panel output stream rather than primary measurement of energy systems.

The full text could not be retrieved. The article is gold open access under CC-BY but is distributed only through ScienceDirect, which refused automated requests, and no repository, preprint or PMC copy exists. This file rests on the publisher-deposited abstract, the Crossref and DOAJ records, and the indexed funding and affiliation metadata, all of which were verified. What could not be checked: whether the body of the paper contains quantitative claims absent from the abstract, and what sources any such figures would rest on. The characterisation above of what the review does not report is drawn from the abstract's own account of its method and conclusions and should be treated as provisional on that basis.

A review that assembles application-level case studies and concludes that the technology is essential reaches that conclusion by construction: no step in the method could have produced the opposite finding, since the corpus surveyed is the corpus of reported successes.
