# AI as Catalyst for Energy Transition: Comprehensive Review of Benefits and Limitations

**Topic:** Narrative - Net Impact Analysis
**Source:** Wang, Li & Li, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esr.2024.101600

## Insight

This comprehensive review positions AI as "not just a tool but a transformative catalyst" for global energy system decarbonization. AI applications span renewable energy deployment, energy efficiency, grid stability, and smart grid integration. The paper identifies AI's pivotal role in accelerating intermittent renewable sources (solar, wind) through advanced forecasting and optimization, and enabling innovations like vehicle-to-grid systems. However, it acknowledges significant challenges: data quality and accessibility, system interoperability, scalability concerns, and privacy/ethics issues. Notably, the paper frames AI's energy footprint as solvable through green AI practices.

## Benefits vs Costs

| Benefits | Costs |
|----------|-------|
| Accelerates renewable energy adoption | Data quality and accessibility challenges |
| Improves energy efficiency through optimization | System interoperability barriers |
| Enables grid stability with variable renewables | Scalability concerns for AI solutions |
| Supports vehicle-to-grid and storage solutions | Privacy and ethical concerns |
| Advanced forecasting reduces energy waste | AI's own energy consumption (acknowledged but minimized) |

## Net Assessment

- **Is AI net positive or negative for environment?** Paper assumes AI is net positive as an "essential" tool for net-zero future. However, this assessment relies on AI's energy efficiency benefits exceeding its own footprint - an assumption not rigorously tested.
- **What's missing from the analysis?** (1) Quantified comparison of AI's energy benefits vs costs (2) Rebound effects from efficiency gains (3) Hardware lifecycle and water consumption (4) Opportunity cost analysis - could resources invested in AI yield better returns elsewhere? (5) Lock-in effects where AI dependence creates new vulnerabilities

## Opportunity Cost Question

With 128 citations, this paper is influential in framing AI as essential for energy transition. But it raises an unasked question: what is the OPPORTUNITY COST?

The billions invested in AI infrastructure (data centers, GPUs, cooling systems) could alternatively fund:
- Direct renewable energy deployment
- Grid infrastructure upgrades
- Energy efficiency retrofits
- Nature-based carbon solutions

Is AI the most carbon-effective use of these resources?
