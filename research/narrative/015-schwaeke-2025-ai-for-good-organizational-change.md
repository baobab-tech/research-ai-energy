# AI for Good? Enabling Organizational Change Towards Sustainability

**Topic:** Narrative - AI Sustainability Claims / Critical Framework
**Source:** Schwaeke, Gerlich, Nguyen, Kanbach & Gast, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11846-025-00840-x
**Journal:** Review of Managerial Science (Springer)
**Citations:** 21

## Insight

This 2025 study critically examines HOW corporations actually leverage AI for sustainability, finding that existing research "lacks a comprehensive exploration" of real-world implementation. Using an inductive concept-development approach with data from 24 companies, the authors identify two key drivers for AI adoption in sustainability: operational enablement and technical capacity. However, the study reveals significant implementation barriers: need for robust data management, system integration challenges, and resistance to AI adoption. The paper proposes an integrative model emphasizing alignment between AI initiatives and sustainability objectives.

## Key Framework

**TOE Framework Application (Technology, Organization, Environment):**

| Dimension | Drivers | Barriers |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| Technology | Technical capacity, data availability | System integration challenges |
| Organization | Operational enablement, strategic alignment | Resistance to AI adoption |
| Environment | Sustainability pressure, competitive advantage | Data management requirements |

## Critical Observations

1. **Research gap confirmed**: "Existing research lacks comprehensive exploration" of how corporations actually use AI for sustainability
2. **Alignment requirement**: AI initiatives must align with sustainability objectives - not automatic
3. **Resistance exists**: Organizations face barriers to AI adoption for sustainability
4. **Data management critical**: Without robust data infrastructure, AI sustainability benefits unrealized

## Net Impact Implications

The paper's framing raises important questions about "AI for good" claims:

1. **Direct vs Indirect Use Cases**: The paper explores both, suggesting direct environmental benefits are not guaranteed
2. **Organizational Barriers**: Even willing companies struggle to implement AI for sustainability effectively
3. **Competitive Advantage Framing**: AI for sustainability often framed as competitive advantage, not environmental imperative
4. **Implementation Gap**: Existence of 24 case studies doesn't mean AI is delivering net environmental benefit

## Missing from Analysis

- AI's own environmental footprint not discussed
- No quantification of actual environmental benefits achieved
- Rebound effects from efficiency gains not considered
- Whether AI adoption delays more direct sustainability actions not examined

## Implication for AI-Climate Narrative

This paper supports the view that "AI for sustainability" is aspirational, not proven. Real-world implementation is complex, faces significant barriers, and requires alignment with sustainability goals that may not occur organically. The competitive advantage framing suggests corporate AI-for-sustainability may prioritize profitability over planetary benefit.
