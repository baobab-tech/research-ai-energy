# AI for Climate Resilience: Systematic Review Reveals Adaptation Dominates, Mitigation Neglected

**Topic:** Narrative - AI for Climate / Net Impact Analysis
**Source:** Frontiers in Climate, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fclim.2025.1585331
**Journal:** Frontiers in Climate
**Citations:** 6

## Insight

This comprehensive 2025 systematic review analyzed 385 peer-reviewed articles (2000-2025) on AI applications for climate resilience. The critical finding: AI research overwhelmingly focuses on adaptation (64.4%) rather than mitigation (16%), with only 19.4% addressing both. This reveals a significant gap in the "AI for climate" narrative - while AI is frequently promoted as a solution for reducing emissions, actual research prioritizes adapting to climate change rather than preventing it. The review also exposes major regional disparities: Asia and global-scale studies account for two-thirds of literature, while Africa and South America are underrepresented.

## Key Statistics

## Sectoral Focus

Agriculture and urban infrastructure receive the most attention. This concentration raises questions about whether "AI for climate" research addresses the largest emission sources (energy, industry, transport) or focuses on more accessible applications.

## Regional Disparities

- **Overrepresented**: Asia, global-scale studies (2/3 of literature)
- **Underrepresented**: Africa, South America
- **Implication**: AI climate solutions may not be developed for regions most vulnerable to climate impacts

## Critical Observations

1. **Adaptation vs Mitigation Gap**: The 4:1 ratio of adaptation to mitigation research suggests AI is being positioned to help us cope with climate change rather than prevent it
2. **Regional inequity**: Vulnerable regions are underserved by AI climate research
3. **Challenges persist**: "Data access, model transparency, and equitable deployment" remain barriers
4. **Ethical concerns**: Paper emphasizes need for "ethical frameworks to ensure AI contributes meaningfully to global climate resilience efforts"

## Net Impact Question

If AI research focuses primarily on adaptation rather than mitigation, the "AI will save the climate" narrative may be misplaced. Adaptation helps manage consequences; it does not reduce emissions. This systematic review suggests the AI-climate research agenda may be misaligned with the urgency of emissions reduction.

## Missing Analysis

- AI's own environmental footprint not addressed
- Economic costs of AI deployment not examined
- No assessment of actual emissions reductions achieved
- Opportunity cost analysis absent
