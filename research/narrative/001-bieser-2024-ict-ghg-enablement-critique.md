# Telecom Industry's GHG "Enablement" Claims Under Scrutiny

**Topic:** Narrative - AI for Climate Claims
**Source:** Bieser, Coroama, Bergmark, Sturmer, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/jiec.13524

## Insight

For about a decade, telecommunication network operators (TNOs) have promoted claims that their customers achieve GHG reductions by using their services (e.g., replacing travel with video conferencing). Some TNOs calculate a "GHG enablement factor" - the ratio of claimed emissions avoided to their own footprint. Since enablements typically exceed the footprint, this creates a narrative that TNOs are net climate positive. This paper systematically analyzes these claims and finds they are methodologically flawed and create a misleading picture of the industry's true climate impact.

## Claims vs Evidence

- **Claim:** Telecom services enable customers to reduce emissions more than the industry's own footprint
- **Evidence:** Analysis reveals several methodological shortcomings: different sets of services considered, inconsistent system boundaries, double counting of reductions, and disregard for rebound effects

- **Claim:** ICT sector is a climate solution
- **Evidence:** TNO assessments focus exclusively on services likely to yield GHG reductions while neglecting possible GHG-increasing services

## Notes

- Authors from ETH Zurich and Ericsson Research
- Provides 8 recommendations for improving methodological basis
- Key critique: current enablement assessments "do not accurately and comprehensively represent TNOs' overall GHG impacts"
- This methodology critique applies directly to AI companies making similar claims about AI-for-climate benefits
- The "enablement factor" approach allows cherry-picking beneficial applications while ignoring harmful ones
