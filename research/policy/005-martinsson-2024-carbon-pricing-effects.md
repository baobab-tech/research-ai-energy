# Carbon Pricing Effectiveness: Swedish CO2 Tax Evidence

**Topic:** Policy - Current Regulation
**Source:** Martinsson, Sajtos, Stromberg, Thomann, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhad097

## Insight

This empirical study of Sweden's carbon tax (introduced 1991) provides rare long-term evidence on carbon pricing effectiveness. Using 26 years of firm-level data, the authors find an "emission-to-pricing elasticity of around two" - meaning carbon pricing significantly reduces emissions. However, effectiveness varies substantially: firms with higher abatement costs and tighter financial constraints show lower responsiveness. The 2015 Swedish manufacturing emissions would have been roughly 30% higher without carbon pricing.

## Key Policies

- **Swedish CO2 Tax**: One of world's first carbon taxes (1991); provides longest track record
- **Emission Reduction**: ~30% lower manufacturing emissions attributed to carbon pricing
- **Heterogeneity**: Effectiveness varies by firm financial constraints and abatement costs
- **Price Signal**: Direct carbon pricing creates measurable emission reduction incentives

## Implications for AI/Data Centers

- Carbon pricing could apply to data center operations where enacted
- High-electricity industries (like AI training) would face significant cost exposure
- Tech firms with strong cash positions may respond more to carbon prices
- Regional variation in carbon pricing creates location arbitrage opportunities for data centers
