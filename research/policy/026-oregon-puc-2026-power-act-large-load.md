# Oregon conditioned data-centre energisation on clean-energy compliance, and declined to impose any efficiency, emissions or water obligation on the facility itself

**Topic:** Policy and regulation: whether any US large-load instrument carries an environmental term, and what the first POWER Act implementation actually requires
**Source:** Public Utility Commission of Oregon, Order No. 26-154, Docket UM 2377 (Investigation into Marginal Cost Study Treatment of Costs for Large Customers and Further Modifications to Portland General Electric Company's Rule C and Rule I)
**Type:** regulatory decision / primary legal document
**URL:** https://apps.puc.state.or.us/orders/2026ords/26-154.pdf
**Published:** 2026-05 (entered 7 May 2026; 77 pages)

## Finding

Oregon is a partial exception to the pattern that US data-centre instruments are purely grid-economic. Order 26-154, the first implementation of HB 3546 (the POWER Act), creates PGE Schedule 96 for data centres of 20 MW and above and adds an interconnection queue under which a Schedule 96 customer is not energised until a reliable plan of service exists that does not impede PGE's compliance with HB 2021, Oregon's statutory requirement that utility emissions fall 80 percent below baseline by 2030, 90 percent by 2035 and 100 percent by 2040. The environmental term operates on the utility's obligation, not on the facility. The Commission expressly declined CRITFC's request for minimum standards for efficiency, backup storage and flexibility as interconnection criteria, holding that the queue and the minimum demand provisions suffice, and expressly declined to collect water-use data, holding that data-centre water is a matter for the Oregon Water Resources Department. Nothing in the order regulates what the data centre emits, how efficiently it runs, or how much water it consumes.

## Methodology

A contested-case order on a full evidentiary record: three rounds of testimony, data requests, an evidentiary hearing on 21 January 2026, and a First Partial Stipulation signed 4 February 2026 by PGE, Staff, AWEC, Amazon Data Services, the Coalition, the Data Center Coalition and CUB. PGE carried the burden of proof under ORS 757.210. Every figure above is a Commission determination, not an estimate.

The statutory route by which climate enters the decision is narrow and traceable. HB 3546 § 2(3) directs the Commission, when approving a large-data-centre schedule, to consider whether the rates impede the electric company's ability to meet the clean energy targets in ORS 469A.410 or reduce greenhouse gas emissions consistent with state policy, and whether they allow for procurement of generation supporting those targets. The Commission read that directive as authority to condition energisation: "Allowing data center customers to connect to the grid without first ensuring that they are served by HB 2021-compliant sources would be ceding that responsibility." PGE, Amazon and AWEC argued the queue violates the obligation to serve; the Commission held that delaying service until it can be provided lawfully is not denial of service, citing PGE's existing discretionary capacity queue.

The 90 percent minimum demand level was derived from a record figure, not from peer practice: PGE's data-centre customers run at a 79 percent load factor, so shared resources serving them are available to others roughly 20 percent of the time, and the Commission set the minimum at the midpoint between PGE's proposed 80 percent and full 100 percent cost responsibility.

## Limitations and conflicts

This is one utility's tariff. It binds PGE, not PacifiCorp or Idaho Power, and the POWER Act's Commission-to-Legislature reporting duty under § 7 remains the only statewide instrument. Existing PGE data-centre contracts are not reopened by the order.

The queue's force is untested. It has no published scoring rule, no defined evidentiary standard for a "reliable plan of service", and no stated timeline; the Commission left tailored, case-by-case solutions to PGE, having been told at hearing that this was feasible. The Commission also recorded the leakage argument without resolving it: CUB agreed data centres could locate elsewhere in Oregon, raising regional costs for PGE customers without paying PGE, and the Commission answered that the risk "does not obviate our duty to see HB 2021's mandates fulfilled". No emissions accounting accompanies the queue, so whether it reduces emissions anywhere depends on siting behaviour the order does not model.

Amazon Data Services and the Data Center Coalition are parties and signatories to the stipulation that set the 20 MW LLCA threshold and the Schedule 96 framework; the contested provisions they lost on are the minimum demand levels, the exceedance penalty, the exit fee and the queue. The Coalition is Climate Solutions, Columbia Riverkeeper, Community Energy Project, the Green Energy Institute at Lewis & Clark Law School and Oregon Environmental Council. CRITFC is the Columbia River Inter-Tribal Fish Commission, and its proposals for efficiency standards, water and emissions reporting, tribal consultation and a tariff pause were all declined.

What the order declines to decide, beyond efficiency standards and water data: a flexible-load subclass (proposed Schedule 96-F, with a 20 MW or 25 percent curtailment criterion); a fast-track queue for flexible loads; a "but-for" analysis in PGE's next Clean Energy Plan / Integrated Resource Plan; direct assignment of the Shute and Evergreen substation costs (a study is ordered for the next rate case instead); allocating the marginal cost of transmission to data centres; treating energy efficiency as a marginal resource in the cost-of-service study or in the Peak Growth Modifier; a minimum generation demand or higher transmission demand for Schedule 89; an exceedance penalty for Schedules 89 and 90; collateral reduction over time; the treatment of exceedance-penalty revenue, deferred to a later amortisation request; and a moratorium on the tariff.

The Commission's own framing of the surcharge is that the costs it offsets are "difficult to quantify" and "difficult to isolate". The 1 cent per kWh figure comes from the Coalition's proposal and is not derived in the order from any estimate of the burden it offsets.

Figures not confirmable from the order text: the dollar value of the system capacity deposit, the exit fee or the collateral per MW, none of which the order reduces to a rate; the number of Schedule 96 customers or megawatts affected; and PGE's queued large-load volume.
