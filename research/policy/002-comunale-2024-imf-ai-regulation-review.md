# An IMF review of AI regulation through early 2024 enumerates six rationales for regulating AI, none environmental, and records AI data-centre energy as a data gap

**Topic:** Policy and regulation — whether environmental impact featured among the stated rationales for AI regulation before the AI Act applied
**Source:** Comunale, M. and Manera, A. (International Monetary Fund), 2024
**Type:** agency report (IMF Working Paper WP/24/65)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.5089/9798400268588.001
**Published:** 2024-03

## Finding

The review sets out the reasons that had driven AI regulation to that date in six subsections: market competition; privacy and copyright; strategic issues and military uses; ethical and bias concerns; financial stability; and effects on political systems. Energy, emissions, water and siting appear in none of them. Environment enters the text at four points only: as a stated aim of the then-provisional EU AI Act, as an example of a UNESCO ethics recommendation, as a gap that a Brookings panel identified in the US Executive Order, and in a single sentence in the research-agenda section stating that "we have only scant data on the increasing energy requirements of AI data centers, which may impact energy markets through prices and investments in additional power sources like nuclear plants." That sentence is supported by a Wall Street Journal article, not by a measurement. The review's other headline result is that regulation has concentrated on topics the economic literature has not studied, and that empirical estimates of AI's employment and productivity effects remain inconclusive.

## Methodology

Narrative review of academic and policy sources through early 2024. The regulation half reads legislative and executive texts for the EU, US and China, and summarises the UK and a set of emerging-market jurisdictions from secondary reporting. No corpus was coded, no instrument count is given, and no quantitative content analysis is performed, so the absence of an environmental rationale reflects the authors' reading of the regulatory debate and not a tally over a defined population of instruments. The paper produces no original data and runs no estimation; every number in it is carried from a cited study.

## Limitations and conflicts

IMF Working Paper; the paper states the views are the authors' and do not represent the IMF, its Executive Board or management. The authors thank an IMF colleague for comments; no external funding is recorded. The cutoff is the binding limitation: the review reads the provisional AI Act text and identifies no energy or environmental documentation obligation in it, so it carries no evidence about what the adopted Regulation requires. The one statement about data-centre energy is an assertion of a data gap with no quantity, no boundary and no primary source. The paper contributes no measurement of AI energy, water or emissions.

## Relation to existing corpus

The review's silence on an AI energy duty is contradicted as a statement about law by 015: Annex XI point 2(e) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 requires GPAI providers to document model energy consumption. The data gap the review flags on data-centre energy is the gap that EED Article 12 reporting began to address in the EU, quantified in 019 and 020.
