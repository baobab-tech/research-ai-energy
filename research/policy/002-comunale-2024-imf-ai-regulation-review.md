# IMF Review: AI Regulation Across Countries

**Topic:** Policy - Current Regulation
**Source:** Comunale, 2024 (International Monetary Fund)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.5089/9798400268588.001

## Insight

This IMF working paper reviews the global landscape of AI regulation, finding that regulations "differ widely in scope and approaches" across countries and face "difficult trade-offs." The paper notes a significant gap: academic research focuses on economic impacts (growth, employment, productivity, inequality) while actual regulation focuses on topics "not explored by academic literature" such as market competition, data privacy, copyright, national security, and ethics. Environmental regulation of AI is notably absent from both domains.

## Key Policies

- **Regulation Coverage**: Market competition, data privacy, copyright, national security, ethics, financial stability
- **Country Variation**: Wide divergence in scope and approach between jurisdictions
- **Trade-offs**: Regulators balance innovation promotion against risk mitigation
- **Enforcement**: Inconsistent across jurisdictions with varying resources and priorities

## Environmental Gap

- Environmental impacts not identified as major regulatory focus area
- No mention of AI energy consumption disclosure requirements
- Carbon pricing for AI operations absent from policy frameworks
- Climate considerations not integrated into AI governance

## Notes

- IMF perspective reflects economic policy focus rather than environmental lens
- Highlights disconnect between academic research and actual policy action
- Empirical evidence on AI effects described as "inconclusive"
- Paper provides framework for understanding WHERE policy attention is (and isn't) directed
