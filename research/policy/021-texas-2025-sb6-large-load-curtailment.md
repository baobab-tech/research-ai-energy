# Texas made remote disconnection of 75 MW+ data centres a condition of grid interconnection

**Topic:** Policy and regulation — enacted US state law on data-centre grid impact
**Source:** Texas Senate Bill 6, 89th Legislature Regular Session (2025); PUCT proposed rule 16 TAC §25.194 (2026)
**Type:** primary legal text / state rulemaking
**URL:** https://capitol.texas.gov/BillLookup/History.aspx?LegSess=89R&Bill=SB6
**Published:** signed and effective 20 June 2025; PUCT rule proposed 12 March 2026

## Finding

SB 6, effective immediately on signature on 20 June 2025, applies to ERCOT loads of 75 MW or more at a single site and conditions interconnection on the installation of equipment allowing the utility to disconnect the load during a firm load shed event. It also requires large-load applicants to disclose whether they are seeking substantially similar interconnection elsewhere in Texas — aimed at phantom load in the queue — to pay transmission screening study fees, and it brings behind-the-meter co-location of generation with large load under regulatory review. The PUCT's implementing rule, proposed as 16 TAC §25.194 on 12 March 2026 with comments due 17 April 2026, adds financial security of $50,000 per MW of requested load, a non-refundable interconnection fee of $50,000 per MW of contracted peak demand, and 100% customer payment of direct interconnection costs.

## Limitations and conflicts

SB 6 is a reliability and cost-allocation statute, not an environmental one. It requires no disclosure of energy consumption, emissions, water use or efficiency, and the disclosure it does require — duplicate interconnection requests — is subject to confidentiality protection. Mandatory curtailment applies to loads interconnecting from January 2026 onward, so the existing ERCOT data-centre fleet is largely outside it. The backup-generation provisions create an incentive to install and run on-site fossil generation during curtailment events, an emissions consequence the statute does not address. Baker Botts, McGuireWoods, Mayer Brown and Sidley analyses used here are law-firm client alerts written for developer audiences.
