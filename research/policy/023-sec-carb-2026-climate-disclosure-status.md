# The SEC moved to rescind its climate rule in 2026 while California's emissions law survived injunction — US mandatory emissions reporting now rests on one state statute

**Topic:** Policy and regulation — whether the corporate emissions numbers this corpus relies on are becoming mandatory
**Source:** US Securities and Exchange Commission, "Rescission of Climate-Related Disclosure Rules" (File S7-2026-19, Release 33-11421); California SB 253 and SB 261 and Ninth Circuit litigation
**Type:** agency rulemaking / primary legal
**URL:** https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/03/2026-11091/rescission-of-climate-related-disclosure-rules
**Published:** SEC proposal voted 29 May 2026, published in the Federal Register 3 June 2026

## Finding

The SEC voted on 29 May 2026 to propose rescinding in full the climate-related disclosure rules it adopted in March 2024, on the stated grounds that they exceed the Commission's statutory authority and impose costs not justified by informational benefits. Comments closed 3 August 2026; no final rescission had issued as of September 2026. In parallel, the Ninth Circuit on 18 November 2025 enjoined enforcement of California SB 261 (climate-related financial risk reporting, >$500m revenue) pending appeal, but denied the same relief against SB 253 (Scope 1 and 2 greenhouse gas reporting, >$1bn revenue). Oral argument was heard 9 January 2026. CARB set an initial SB 253 reporting deadline of 10 August 2026 for Scopes 1 and 2.

## Methodology

## Limitations and conflicts

Whether the Ninth Circuit ruled after January 2026, and whether the 10 August 2026 SB 253 deadline was met or moved, could not be established from sources retrieved — treat the SB 253 filing outcome as unverified. The consequence for this corpus is concrete: the Scope 1/2/3 figures in hyperscaler sustainability reports remain, in the US, voluntary and unaudited for the federal filer population, with SB 253 the only mandatory hook, applying at entity level and not at data-centre or workload level. No US instrument in force requires an operator to disclose energy, water or emissions for a specific facility or for AI workloads.
