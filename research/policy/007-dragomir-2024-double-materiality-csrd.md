# Double Materiality Disclosure Under CSRD: Assessment Process and Requirements

**Topic:** Policy - Proposed Regulation
**Source:** Dragomir, V.D., Dumitru, M., Chersan, I.-C., Gorgan, C., & Paunescu, M., 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1080/17449480.2024.2339264

## Insight

The Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) 2022/2464 introduces mandatory "double materiality" assessment in sustainability reporting across the European Union starting in 2024. This requires companies to disclose both their impacts on environment/society (inside-out) AND how sustainability risks affect the company financially (outside-in). Research on Romanian companies shows most firms currently report primarily positive environmental impacts while underreporting financial materiality, suggesting significant compliance gaps as requirements become binding.

## Proposed Requirements

- **Double materiality assessment:** Mandatory dual-perspective analysis of sustainability impacts and risks
- **Scope:** Direct and indirect impacts, risks, and opportunities across environmental and social dimensions
- **Stakeholder engagement:** Continuous engagement process required (currently most companies rely on isolated events)
- **Governance structures:** Dedicated internal oversight mechanisms must be implemented
- **Timeline:** Phased implementation beginning 2024 for large public-interest entities
- **Jurisdiction:** European Union, with requirements extending to non-EU companies with significant EU presence

## Implementation Challenges Identified

- Most companies currently emphasize positive impacts over negative ones
- Financial materiality assessments remain underdeveloped
- Stakeholder engagement processes not yet continuous
- Gap between current voluntary practices and mandatory requirements

## Relevance to AI Environmental Impact

Double materiality requirements will force tech companies to disclose both environmental impacts (energy consumption, carbon emissions, water use) AND how climate risks affect their business models. This creates regulatory pressure for comprehensive AI environmental accounting that goes beyond current voluntary disclosures.
