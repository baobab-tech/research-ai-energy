# FERC ordered all six RTOs to justify or rewrite their large-load interconnection tariffs, defining a large load as 50 MW above 69 kV

**Topic:** Policy and regulation — US federal action on data-centre grid interconnection
**Source:** Federal Energy Regulatory Commission, show cause orders under FPA section 206, 18 June 2026; Docket RM26-4-000 (ANOPR)
**Type:** agency proceeding
**URL:** https://www.bracewell.com/resources/ferc-show-cause-orders-data-center-interconnection/
**Source note:** FERC docket page: https://www.ferc.gov/rm26-4 — ferc.gov refuses automated retrieval
**Published:** 2026-06-18

## Finding

On 18 June 2026 FERC issued show cause orders under section 206 of the Federal Power Act to PJM, MISO, SPP, CAISO, ISO-NE and NYISO and their transmission owners, requiring each to justify its existing tariff treatment of large and co-located loads or propose revisions. FERC defined a large load as a new commercial or industrial customer at a single site with peak demand of 50 MW or greater interconnecting at transmission voltage above 69 kV, and excluded co-location arrangements from that definition, treating them as a separate category. Rather than a single national rulemaking on the ANOPR record in Docket RM26-4-000 — opened at the direction of the Secretary of Energy on 23 October 2025 — FERC proceeded region by region. Compliance filings were due within 60 days, by 17 August 2026, with a resource adequacy report due within 30 days, by 20 July 2026.

## Methodology

Description of the orders taken from law-firm analyses of the order text; FERC's own pages return HTTP 403 to automated retrieval, so the primary documents were not read directly. Docket numbers EL26-67 (PJM), EL26-70 (MISO), EL26-68 (SPP), EL26-71 (CAISO), EL26-72 (ISO-NE), EL26-69 (NYISO) come from a second, independent law-firm summary and are consistent across sources; the citations reported are 195 FERC ¶ 61,211–61,216.

## Limitations and conflicts

Sources are client alerts written by firms representing developers and utilities, not neutral reporting; the docket numbers and thresholds agree across at least three of them but have not been checked against FERC eLibrary. Substantively, this is a cost-allocation and speed-to-power proceeding: it addresses who pays for network upgrades and how fast load can connect, not emissions, water or efficiency. The threshold gap matters — the ANOPR asked about 20 MW loads, the June orders apply at 50 MW, so a band of mid-sized data centres falls outside the region-by-region reform. Excluding co-location from the large-load definition leaves the behind-the-meter nuclear and gas arrangements that prompted the original PJM co-location dispute (Docket EL25-49) in a separate track.
