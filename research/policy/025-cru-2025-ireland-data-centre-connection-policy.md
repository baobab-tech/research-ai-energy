# Ireland replaced its de facto data-centre connection moratorium with a matching-generation and 80% renewable condition

**Topic:** Policy and regulation — codified data-centre connection restrictions
**Source:** Commission for Regulation of Utilities (Ireland), Large Energy User connection policy decision, CRU2025236
**Type:** regulatory decision
**URL:** https://cruie-live-96ca64acab2247eca8a850a7e54b-5b34f62.divio-media.com/documents/CRU2025236_Large_Energy_User_connection_policy_decision_paper.pdf
**Published:** 2025-12-12

## Finding

The CRU's decision of 12 December 2025 ends the connection criteria that since 2021 had functioned as a de facto moratorium on new data-centre connections in Dublin, and replaces them with conditions. New data centres must provide generation and/or storage capacity, on site or locally, matching their requested maximum import capacity, and must source at least 80% of annual demand from additional renewable electricity projects generating in the Republic of Ireland, with a six-year glide path and a credible development plan presented to the system operators. System operators must assess each application by whether its specific location is constrained or unconstrained rather than applying a regional rule, and were required to publish an engagement and connection process by 31 March 2026.

## Key Data

| Condition | Value |
|---|---|
| Decision date | 12 December 2025 |
| Generation/storage requirement | Capacity matching requested maximum import demand capacity, on-site or local |
| Renewable requirement | ≥80% of annual demand from additional renewable projects in the Republic of Ireland |
| Glide path | 6 years from connection to renewable projects generating |
| Locational test | Constrained vs unconstrained assessed per application, not per region |
| System operator process publication | By 31 March 2026 |
| Dublin context | Data centres account for roughly 50% of regional electricity demand |

## Methodology

Regulatory decision text summarised from the CRU's own decision announcement and decision paper. The "additional" qualifier on renewables is the operative word — the decision requires new generation rather than certificate purchase — but the enforcement mechanism for the six-year glide path was not established from the material retrieved.

## Limitations and conflicts

The matching-capacity requirement can be satisfied with on-site gas generation or batteries as well as renewables, so it is a system-adequacy condition, not a decarbonisation condition, and it creates a standing fleet of on-site fossil capacity co-located with load. The 80% renewable requirement has a six-year lag, during which a connected facility draws from the grid on ordinary terms. Irish regulators are reported to be split on the approach. The CRU is an economic regulator with a security-of-supply mandate; the decision is framed around grid constraint, not emissions or water.
