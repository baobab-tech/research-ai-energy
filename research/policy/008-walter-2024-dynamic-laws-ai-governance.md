# Managing the AI Race: Proposed "Dynamic Laws" Regulatory Model

**Topic:** Policy - Proposed Regulation
**Source:** Walter, Y., 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s44163-024-00109-4

## Insight

This paper proposes an innovative "dynamic laws" regulatory model to address the gap between rapid AI advancement and slower regulatory processes. The analysis examines AI governance across the US, EU, Asia, Africa, and the Americas, finding that current frameworks struggle to keep pace with technological change. The proposed model advocates for a collaborative network blending governmental authority with industry expertise to establish adaptive, responsive regulations that can evolve with technological advancements.

## Proposed Requirements

- **Dynamic regulatory framework:** Laws that can automatically update as technology evolves
- **Collaborative governance:** Joint government-industry regulatory bodies
- **Adaptive standards:** Regulations designed to respond to technological changes without requiring full legislative cycles
- **Sector-specific considerations:** Recognition that AI impacts vary across employment, information integrity, and democratic stability
- **Multi-stakeholder approach:** Balance between governmental oversight and industry self-regulation

## Key Policy Gaps Identified

- Current regulations lag behind rapid AI development
- Ethical standard-setting moves slower than innovation
- Tension between government regulation and industry expertise
- Global regulatory fragmentation across jurisdictions

## Relevance to AI Environmental Impact

The "dynamic laws" concept is highly relevant for AI environmental regulation, where energy consumption, efficiency standards, and carbon accounting methodologies evolve rapidly with new hardware and software developments. Static regulations risk becoming obsolete, while adaptive frameworks could better track evolving AI sustainability challenges.
