# ESG Regulation and Climate Disclosure: The CSRD Framework

**Topic:** Policy - Current Regulation
**Source:** Oliver Yebenes, 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s40822-023-00251-x

## Insight

The European Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) is pushing ESG indicators to become "a key set of information" for financial reporting. The paper analyzes how ESG criteria are becoming mandatory requirements in financial markets, with regulations pushing corporations to reflect their "social and ecological footprint" in external reporting. Credit rating agencies are becoming important actors in sustainability assessments, transmitting ESG importance to investors and markets.

## Key Policies

- **EU CSRD**: Mandates sustainability reporting for large companies; requires disclosure of environmental, social, and governance impacts
- **ESG Disclosure**: Moving from voluntary to mandatory framework in EU
- **Credit Rating Integration**: Rating agencies incorporating ESG risks into assessments
- **Financial Materiality**: ESG factors increasingly considered material to financial performance

## Relevance to AI/Tech

- CSRD will require large tech companies operating in EU to report environmental impacts
- Data center operators subject to sustainability disclosure requirements
- Scope 3 emissions (supply chain) reporting could capture AI training/inference carbon
- Framework exists for mandatory disclosure but AI-specific requirements not yet codified

## Notes

- Paper focuses on financial sector ESG adoption, not AI-specific
- Highlights "huge demand" for non-financial indicators that "do not have same profoundness" as traditional metrics
- Tech companies will need to adapt to dual financial/sustainability reporting
- Transition period for companies to "adapt both worlds" and "make them join and connect together"
