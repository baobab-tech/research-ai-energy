# Generative AI Policy Frameworks: EU, US, UK Comparison

**Topic:** Policy - Current Regulation
**Source:** Capraro, Lentsch, Acemoglu et al., 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae191

## Insight

This interdisciplinary paper with 33 authors including Daron Acemoglu evaluates AI policy frameworks across the EU, US, and UK. The authors find that current policies fail to fully address socioeconomic challenges from generative AI. The EU AI Act takes a risk-based approach, while the US relies on sector-specific guidance and executive orders, and the UK favors regulatory sandboxes. None adequately address environmental impacts of AI deployment.

## Key Policies

- **EU AI Act**: Risk-based framework categorizing AI systems; focuses on safety, privacy, and fairness; limited environmental provisions
- **US Approach**: Executive orders, NIST AI Risk Management Framework, sector-specific agency guidance; fragmented without comprehensive federal law
- **UK Approach**: Regulatory sandboxes and principles-based guidance; emphasis on innovation over precautionary regulation
- **US-EU Trade and Technology Council**: Voluntary AI code of conduct for international coordination

## Gaps Identified

- Environmental sustainability not central to any major framework
- Disclosure requirements for AI energy/water use not mandated
- Carbon footprint reporting not required for AI systems
- Focus remains on safety, privacy, bias rather than environmental impact

## Notes

- Paper explicitly calls for policies to "reduce inequalities while mitigating harmful effects"
- Identifies tension between innovation promotion and environmental protection
- Highly cited (205 citations) interdisciplinary collaboration
- Environmental impact addressed primarily as economic externality, not core regulatory concern
