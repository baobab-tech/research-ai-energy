# ESG Reporting Gaps: Path to Sustainable Development

**Topic:** Policy - Current Regulation
**Source:** Chopra, Senadheera, Dissanayake et al., 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/su16020606

## Insight

Despite ESG reporting becoming a "global standard in financial markets," persistent concerns surround the quality of ESG reporting and its tangible impact on Sustainable Development. The paper identifies a critical gap: "scarcity of interdisciplinary expertise across diversified fields" prevents robust reporting mechanisms capable of encompassing sustainability's multifaceted nature. Current company-centric ESG reporting needs to extend beyond firm boundaries to capture full environmental and social impacts.

## Key Policies

- **Current State**: ESG reporting as global financial standard, but quality concerns persist
- **SEA Framework**: Social and Environmental Accounting proposed as structured approach
- **Standardization Gap**: Sustainability metrics lack consistent standards across jurisdictions
- **Company vs. System**: Reporting remains company-centric, missing broader system impacts

## Implications for AI Disclosure

- AI environmental impacts cross organizational boundaries (cloud providers, chip manufacturers, power utilities)
- Current ESG frameworks struggle to capture full lifecycle impacts
- Standardized metrics for AI energy/carbon/water consumption not yet established
- Stakeholder demand for AI environmental transparency growing but reporting mechanisms immature
