# AI Governance: A Systematic Literature Review

**Topic:** Policy - AI Governance Frameworks
**Source:** Batool, Zowghi & Bano, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-024-00653-w

## Insight

This systematic review from CSIRO Australia analyzed 28 articles to map the landscape of AI governance frameworks, addressing WHO is accountable, WHAT is governed, WHEN governance occurs in the AI lifecycle, and HOW it is implemented. The study categorizes governance at five levels: team, organization, industry, national, and international. While providing a comprehensive mapping of accountability structures, the analysis reveals that existing AI governance frameworks focus heavily on ethical risks (bias, privacy, safety) while environmental sustainability remains largely absent from formal governance frameworks.

## Key Data

- **59 citations** in 2025 (high impact)
- **28 articles** analyzed through systematic review
- **5 governance levels** identified: team, organization, industry, national, international
- **4 research questions** addressed: WHO, WHAT, WHEN, HOW of AI governance
- **Publisher:** AI and Ethics journal

## Governance Framework Gaps

The study identifies that various governance frameworks exist from governments, organizations, and companies, but:
- Environmental impact is not a primary governance concern
- Energy consumption and carbon emissions lack standardized governance
- Water usage not mentioned as governance parameter
- Focus remains on algorithmic risks, not infrastructure impacts
- No framework specifically addresses AI hardware lifecycle governance

## Relevance to AI Environmental Policy

This review confirms that even as AI governance matures as a field, environmental sustainability is being systematically overlooked. The absence of energy/carbon/water governance in formal AI frameworks means environmental accountability relies on general ESG regulations rather than AI-specific requirements.
