## EU: a duty without a measurement standard

The AI Act's Annex XI 2(e) energy-documentation duty became applicable on 2 August 2025. The
Article 40(2) harmonised standards that would define how to measure have not been delivered, as
the Bundesnetzagentur states directly ([017](017-bnetza-2026-no-harmonised-standard-ai-energy-efficiency.md)).
The Commission opened its consultation on how to measure AI energy in April 2026, industry
respondents only, scoping a potential AI energy label ([018](018-ec-2026-consultation-measuring-ai-energy-label.md)).

What the GPAI Model Documentation Form asks for ([016](016-ec-2025-gpai-code-of-practice-model-documentation-form.md)):

| Item | Unit | Disclosed to |
|---|---|---|
| Training energy | MWh, 2 significant figures | AI Office and national authorities |
| Inference | floating point operations | AI Office and national authorities |

Nothing is published. All of it falls under Article 78 trade-secret protection. "N/A" is
permitted for training energy where a compute or hardware provider withholds data, covering any
model trained on rented capacity. The duty produces no public comparable figure and records no
operational inference energy.

## EU: the one working disclosure regime

EED Article 12 with Delegated Regulation 2024/1364 requires annual reporting above 500 kW IT
load ([019](019-eu-2024-eed-article-12-delegated-regulation-1364.md)). The first round
([020](020-dg-ener-2025-first-eu-data-centre-reporting-round.md)):

- 770 data centres, about 36% coverage; six member states reported nothing
- Ireland returned 18 of ~123 facilities; Germany returned 335 of ~456
- 14,088 GWh, 3,738.86 MW installed IT power, 6,223,391 m³ water
- Weighted PUE 1.36 (n=681), from 1.64 at 500 kW to 1.21 above 10 MW
- WUE 0.58 m³/MWh (n=458); renewable factor 0.87, inflated by Guarantees of Origin with no
  additionality test

Facility-level data is confidential under Article 5(5), so no figure can be attributed to a
named operator.

## US: grid economics, not environment

Enacted instruments govern curtailment and cost allocation and contain no energy, water or
emissions term.

- **Texas SB 6**, effective 20 June 2025: remote-disconnection equipment as a condition of ERCOT
  interconnection at 75 MW. Proposed PUCT rule adds $50,000/MW security and a $50,000/MW
  non-refundable interconnection fee ([021](021-texas-2025-sb6-large-load-curtailment.md)).
- **FERC**, 18 June 2026: show cause orders to all six RTOs, defining a large load as 50 MW above
  69 kV, covering cost allocation, flexible service and co-location
  ([022](022-ferc-2026-large-load-show-cause-orders.md)).
- **States**, 2026: Virginia enacted cost allocation at 25 MW. Energy and water **disclosure**
  bills in California, Georgia, South Carolina, Kansas, Iowa and Michigan were introduced and
  failed to pass ([024](024-us-states-2026-data-centre-legislation.md)).

US mandatory emissions reporting now rests on one state statute. The SEC voted on 29 May 2026 to
propose full rescission of its 2024 climate rule. SB 261 was enjoined; SB 253 survived, with
CARB setting 10 August 2026 for first Scope 1 and 2 filings
([023](023-sec-carb-2026-climate-disclosure-status.md)).

Ireland's CRU ended the de facto Dublin moratorium in December 2025, conditioning connection on
matching on-site generation or storage ([025](025-cru-2025-ireland-data-centre-connection-policy.md)).
