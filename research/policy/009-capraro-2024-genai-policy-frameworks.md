# Generative AI Policy Making: Comparative Analysis of EU, US, and UK Frameworks

**Topic:** Policy - Proposed Regulation
**Source:** Capraro, V. et al. (33 authors), 2024
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae191

## Insight

This interdisciplinary analysis by 33 experts examines policy frameworks in the European Union, United States, and United Kingdom for addressing generative AI impacts on work, education, and healthcare. The study finds that no major framework adequately confronts the socioeconomic challenges AI poses, including environmental sustainability concerns. The paper proposes concrete policies to promote shared prosperity through AI advancement, emphasizing that current regulatory approaches fail to fully address the complex trade-offs involved.

## Proposed Policy Framework Analysis

**European Union:**
- Risk-based approach in AI Act focuses on safety, transparency, and human oversight
- Strengths: Comprehensive regulatory framework, extraterritorial reach
- Weaknesses: May stifle innovation, slow adaptation to technological change

**United States:**
- Sector-specific, decentralized approach
- Strengths: Flexibility, supports innovation
- Weaknesses: Fragmented, inconsistent protections

**United Kingdom:**
- "Pro-innovation" approach with sector-specific regulators
- Strengths: Agile, context-sensitive
- Weaknesses: Potential regulatory gaps, less comprehensive

## Key Policy Recommendations

- Explicit consideration of trade-offs in policy design
- Interdisciplinary collaboration in governance
- Proactive measures to mitigate inequality amplification
- Adaptive frameworks that can evolve with technology

## Environmental Gap

The analysis notably highlights that existing frameworks focus primarily on socioeconomic impacts, with environmental sustainability concerns (energy consumption, carbon footprint) receiving insufficient policy attention across all three jurisdictions.
