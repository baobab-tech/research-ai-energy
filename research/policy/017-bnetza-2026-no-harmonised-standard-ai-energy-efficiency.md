# A national AI Act regulator states that no standard defines how to document AI energy efficiency

**Topic:** Policy and regulation — whether the AI Act's energy-efficiency standardisation mandate has produced anything enforceable
**Source:** Bundesnetzagentur (German Federal Network Agency), AI Service Desk — "Energy and resource efficiency"
**Type:** official guidance / national competent authority
**URL:** https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/EN/Areas/Digitalisation/AI/12_EnergyEfficiency/start.html
**Published:** page current as retrieved 2026-09-19

## Finding

Article 40(2) of the AI Act obliges the Commission to issue standardisation requests covering "reporting and documentation processes to improve AI systems' resource performance, such as reducing the high-risk AI system's consumption of energy and of other resources during its lifecycle, and on the energy-efficient development of general-purpose AI models." Germany's designated AI Act market surveillance authority states plainly that the resulting standards do not exist: "The standards to be developed in response to the above-mentioned standardisation request have not yet been delivered. The detailed requirements for documenting the resource and energy efficiency of AI have therefore not yet been defined." In the absence of a standard, the agency points providers to three non-binding research projects (Green AI Guidelines, SustAIn, SustainML).

## Key Data

| Item | Status as of 2026-09 |
|---|---|
| AI Act Art. 40(2) standardisation request on energy/resource efficiency | Issued to CEN/CENELEC; deliverables outstanding |
| CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 delivery | Autumn 2025 deadline missed; full suite not expected before December 2026 |
| Art. 53(5) delegated act on measurement/calculation methodologies | Not adopted |
| Binding metric, unit or boundary for AI energy documentation | None in force |
| Authority's substitute guidance | Green AI Guidelines, SustAIn, SustainML — all voluntary research outputs |

## Methodology

Statement of legal status by a national competent authority, read against Article 40(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The JTC 21 timing is drawn from CEN-CENELEC's own communications as reported in the standards-tracking literature and is the weakest link in this excerpt; the Bundesnetzagentur statement of non-delivery is direct.

## Limitations and conflicts

The Bundesnetzagentur page is guidance, not law, and does not bind other member states' authorities. It does not say when standards are expected. The Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) tied the application of high-risk obligations to the availability of compliance support measures including harmonised standards, and deferred Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 — so the standards gap has a direct, documented regulatory consequence for the high-risk limb, while the GPAI limb (Annex XI point 2(e)) stayed applicable without a standard behind it.
