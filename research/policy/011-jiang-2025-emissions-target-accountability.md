# Limited Accountability for Corporate Emissions Targets

**Topic:** Policy - ESG Accountability Gap
**Source:** Jiang, Kim & Lu, 2025
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41558-024-02236-3

## Insight

This Nature Climate Change study reveals a critical accountability gap in corporate emissions targets: companies are rewarded for announcements but not penalized for failures. Of 1,041 firms with emissions targets ending in 2020, 9% explicitly failed and 31% simply "disappeared" with no public outcome. Only THREE failed targets received media coverage. The market shows no significant negative reaction to missed targets, no change in media sentiment, and no decline in environmental scores. This creates perverse incentives: firms gain from announcing ambitious targets with no consequences for missing them.

## Key Data

- **1,041 firms** had emissions targets ending in 2020
- **88 (9%)** explicitly failed their targets
- **320 (31%)** targets simply "disappeared" - no disclosure of outcome
- **Only 3** failed targets received media coverage
- **No significant market reaction** observed after firms fail targets
- **No decline in ESG scores** after target failures
- **No increase in shareholder proposals** targeting failed firms

## Critical Finding

Initial target announcements ARE rewarded with positive media sentiment and improved ESG scores. This asymmetry - rewards for pledges, no penalty for failures - creates systematic incentives for corporate greenwashing. The authors explicitly raise concerns for 2030 and 2050 targets, which are more ambitious and further from accountability.

## Relevance to AI/Tech Policy

Tech companies' "carbon neutral by 2030" and "water positive by 2030" pledges face the same accountability vacuum. This study predicts that missed AI/data center environmental targets will likely face no consequences, reinforcing the need for mandatory verification and penalty mechanisms.
