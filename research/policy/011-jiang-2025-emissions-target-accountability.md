# Of 1,041 firms with emissions targets expiring in 2020, 88 failed and 320 stopped reporting the target; three failures were covered by the media and none drew a market reaction

**Topic:** Policy — whether corporate climate targets carry consequences when missed
**Source:** Jiang, Kim & Lu, 2025
**Type:** peer-reviewed
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41558-024-02236-3
**Published:** 2025-01

## Finding

Emissions targets expiring in 2020 were examined at expiry rather than by extrapolating progress. Of 1,041
firms, 633 (60.8%) achieved, 88 (8.5%) failed, and 320 (30.7%) disappeared, meaning the firm had disclosed
a 2020 target in an earlier CDP filing and disclosed no outcome for it by 2021. Three of the 88 failed
firms received any media coverage of the failure, and all three of those traced to the firm's own
acknowledgement rather than independent reporting. No coverage at all was found for any disappeared
target. Failing produced no significant abnormal return, no abnormal trading volume, no change in media
sentiment, no fall in environmental scores and no increase in environment-related shareholder proposals.
The initial announcement of the same targets did produce significant improvements in media sentiment and
environmental scores.

## Methodology

Targets are identified from CDP, restricted to long-term goals covering at least 80% of a firm's scope 1 or
scope 2 emissions with a duration over three years. Outcomes are read at expiry, which avoids assuming
linear progress and makes disappearance observable as a distinct category. Transparency is measured through
sustainability reports, press releases and news coverage. Consequences are tested by event study on three
information events, plus media sentiment, ESG environmental scores from ISS, and shareholder proposals.
Disappeared firms are split into leaders and laggards by industry-adjusted emissions reduction rate.
Supplementary analysis tests four alternative explanations for the absence of consequences and reports that
none accounts for it. Financial data are from Datastream; code is archived at Zenodo.

## Limitations and conflicts

The authors declare no competing interests, and no funding from a regulated or rated entity is disclosed.
The acknowledgements list academic seminar audiences only.

The sample is firms that disclosed to CDP, which are larger, more scrutinised and more likely to follow
through than firms that disclose nothing. The accountability gap measured here is therefore a lower bound.

"Disappeared" pools two behaviours the paper cannot fully separate: silently dropping a target that looked
likely to fail, and replacing it with a more ambitious target further out. The 63.2% shortfall figure
applies only to the subset with absolute targets and disclosed emissions.

Null results on market reaction are statements about detectable effect within the event windows used, not
proof of indifference. The paper addresses this by testing several outcome channels, all of which return
nulls.

The study covers targets that expired in 2020. Its bearing on 2030 and 2050 targets is inferential, which
the authors state directly.

## Relation to existing corpus

Supplies the base rate against which corporate AI and data-centre climate pledges should be read. The
information technology sector in this sample shows 26 disappeared targets against 8 formal failures, so
the dominant failure mode is silence rather than admission. This bears on the restated and withdrawn
targets documented in /research/washing/026 and on the scope 2 accounting gaps in /research/washing/018
and /research/washing/020: the finding here is that no stakeholder channel penalised any of it.
