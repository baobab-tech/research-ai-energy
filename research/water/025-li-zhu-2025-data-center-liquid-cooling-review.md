# Non-Uniform Load Distribution and Cooling Solutions in Data Centers: Liquid vs Air

**Topic:** Water Consumption - Cooling Technology Trade-offs
**Source:** Li, Zhu, Li, Yang (2025)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.3390/en18010149

## Insight

This 2025 review in Energies examines the critical trade-off between micro-scale liquid cooling and large-scale air cooling in data centers. As chip power density reaches 10^6-10^7 W/m^2, traditional air cooling becomes insufficient, driving adoption of water-based cooling that increases water consumption. The paper analyzes microchannel heat sink (MCHS) technologies that can reduce but not eliminate water dependence.

## Key Technical Findings

| Cooling Method | Water Implications |
|----------------|-------------------|
| Direct expansion (air) | Low water use, but insufficient for high-density AI chips |
| Water-cooled chillers | Effective cooling, but high water consumption |
| Microchannel liquid cooling | Most efficient heat transfer, requires water-based coolants |
| Flow boiling systems | Enhanced cooling capacity, but stability concerns |

## Water-Relevant Insights

- **Non-uniform heat distribution** on chips creates local hot spots requiring intensive cooling
- **Microchannel heat sinks (MCHS)** offer best performance but depend on liquid coolants
- **Multi-scale synergy** approach recommended: combining micro (chip-level) and macro (room-level) cooling
- **AI integration** for intelligent cooling control could optimize water usage

## Relevance to AI Water Footprint

The paper explains WHY AI data centers are water-intensive:
1. GPU/accelerator chips generate extreme heat densities
2. Air cooling cannot handle these thermal loads
3. Liquid cooling (water-based) becomes necessary
4. More powerful AI chips = more cooling = more water
