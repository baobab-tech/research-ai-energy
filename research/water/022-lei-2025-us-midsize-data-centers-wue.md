# U.S. Small/Midsize Data Centers: More Energy-Efficient but More Water-Intensive

**Topic:** Water Consumption - Data Center WUE Trade-offs
**Source:** Lei, Ganeshalingam, Masanet, Smith, Shehabi (2025)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enbuild.2025.115734

## Insight

This 2025 study using CBECS survey data reveals a critical trade-off in data center sustainability: small and midsize U.S. data centers are becoming MORE energy-efficient through economizer adoption but simultaneously MORE water-intensive due to widespread use of water-cooled chillers. The study provides the first comprehensive WUE (Water Usage Effectiveness) estimates by facility size and year for this underexamined segment that still houses 40% of U.S. servers.

## Key Data

| Metric | Value | Context |
|--------|-------|---------|
| Small/midsize DC decline | 1.764M to 1.398M facilities | 2012-2018 (21% decline) |

| Servers in office buildings | 55% | Of small/midsize data centers |
| Direct expansion cooling | Dominant | But transitioning to more efficient solutions |
| Water-cooled chillers | Widespread | In small/midsize facilities specifically |
| PUE range | 1.15-1.43 | Varies by climate zone and cooling strategy |

## Critical Finding

**"Midsize data centers are but more energy-efficient but more water-intensive due to the widespread use of water-cooled chillers."**

This represents a hidden water burden: while hyperscale facilities get public scrutiny, small and midsize data centers (in office buildings, enterprise facilities) are quietly consuming disproportionate amounts of water per compute unit due to legacy cooling infrastructure.

## Relevance to AI Water Research

- Illustrates that aggregate water consumption patterns depend heavily on facility mix
- 40% of servers in older, potentially more water-intensive facilities
- Energy efficiency improvements (economizers) don't automatically reduce water use
- Climate zone determines 24% efficiency variation - geography matters for water footprint
- Authors provide first quantified WUE estimates for non-hyperscale facilities
