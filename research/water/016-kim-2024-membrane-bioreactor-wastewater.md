# Recent Advances of Membrane-Based Hybrid Membrane Bioreactors for Wastewater Reclamation

**Topic:** Water Consumption - Reuse/Recycling
**Source:** Kim, Wu, Jeong, Jeong & Kim (2024)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.3389/frmst.2024.1361433

## Insight

This review examines advanced membrane bioreactor (MBR) technologies for wastewater reclamation, including integration with desalination technologies applicable to industrial water recycling. The paper identifies that conventional MBRs are limited for wastewater reuse applications, but hybrid systems combining MBR with nanofiltration (NF), reverse osmosis (RO), forward osmosis (FO), or membrane distillation (MD) can produce high-quality water suitable for industrial cooling applications. For data centers, these technologies enable on-site wastewater treatment and recycling for cooling systems.

## Key Data

- MBR technology established for 3+ decades in wastewater treatment
- Hybrid MBR systems achieve superior effluent quality for reuse
- Low energy consumption with FO and MD technologies
- Excellent rejection efficiency for solid materials and contaminants

## Reuse Technologies

- **Conventional MBR:** Limited for reuse applications; good for pretreatment
- **MBR + Nanofiltration (NF):** Enhanced effluent quality
- **MBR + Reverse Osmosis (RO):** High-quality permeate for demanding applications
- **MBR + Forward Osmosis (FO):** Low energy, high rejection efficiency
- **MBR + Membrane Distillation (MD):** Excellent rejection, low energy
- **Microbial activity consideration:** Biological treatment integration

## Challenges

- Fouling control in membrane systems
- Energy consumption for pressurized systems (NF/RO)
- Technical limitations for FO and MD commercialization
- Retentate stream recovery and concentrate management

## Relevance to Data Centers

- On-site wastewater treatment enables circular water economy
- Hybrid MBR systems can produce cooling tower make-up water quality
- Low-energy options (FO, MD) reduce operational footprint
- Modular systems can scale with data center water demands
