# Microsoft FY25: 13,266 ML Withdrawn, 8,170 ML Consumed, 14.2 Million m3 Replenished — "Water Positive" Reached in Aggregate, Not Watershed by Watershed

**Topic:** Water — corporate disclosure FY2025; withdrawal vs consumption; water-positive pledge accounting; site-level WUE
**Source:** Microsoft, 2026 Environmental Sustainability Report and 2026 Environmental Data Fact Sheet (fiscal year ended 30 June 2025)
**Type:** corporate disclosure
**URL:** https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/2026-Microsoft-Environmental-Data-Fact-Sheet-PDF.pdf
**Source note:** report: https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/2026-Microsoft-Environmental-Sustainability-Report-PDF.pdf
**Published:** 2026-07-09

## Finding

Microsoft announced that FY25 replenishment (14.2 million m3, third-party quantified) exceeded global withdrawals (13.266 million m3) for the first time. The site-level table published for the first time in this report shows how that aggregate is composed: of 28 disclosed datacenter locations, seven have any delivered replenishment volume, and replenishment is concentrated where withdrawal is small — Querétaro, Mexico replenishes 59x its own withdrawal and Madrid 34x, while Boydton VA (362 ML), Ashburn VA (239 ML), Chicago (204 ML) and Dublin (18 ML) have delivered none. Microsoft states the intention to move to a watershed-by-watershed basis, which is an acknowledgement that the current claim is a global sum. Site water intensity spans roughly 70x across the fleet, from 0.014 L/kWh (Dublin) to 1.03 L/kWh (Phoenix).

## Methodology

Water inventory covers assets under operational control — owned and leased offices, datacenters and labs — on a GRI 303 (2018) basis: withdrawal is total water taken from any source. Site-level withdrawal comes primarily from utility bills; where primary data is unavailable, Microsoft estimates from electricity consumption and cooling technology type. Locations collectively under 1% of total owned-datacenter water and electricity are excluded, as is commissioning activity. Replenishment volume is "quantified by third parties across our replenishment project portfolio" (no methodology or verifier named in the Fact Sheet).

Deloitte & Touche performed a **review** engagement (not an audit) on management's assertion for Section 1 as of FY25 only. Prior-year figures, forward-looking statements, goals and progress against goals — which includes the water-positive claim — were explicitly outside that review.

## Limitations and conflicts

Self-reported by the company whose pledge is being assessed; assurance is limited-scope review, and the water-positive progress claim sits outside it.

Specific accounting gaps:
- **Withdrawal/consumption split by source is unavailable.** Microsoft states the freshwater/other-water breakdown for withdrawals, the breakdown for discharges, and third-party withdrawal sources in water-stressed areas are all currently not reported. So the 50%-in-stressed-areas figure cannot be resolved into freshwater vs reclaimed.
- **Site table reports withdrawal only** — no site-level consumption, so per-site depletion is not recoverable. The non-potable percentage is blank for most sites.
- **Watershed and timing matching are not demonstrated.** Aggregate replenishment exceeds aggregate withdrawal, but the site table shows the two are not co-located. The 133 million m3 contracted figure is a lifetime total against annual withdrawals; only the 14.2 million m3 is annual.
- **Indirect (generation) water is entirely absent.** Everything here is on-site. On LBNL's 12:1 indirect:direct consumption ratio, Microsoft's off-site water dependency would dwarf the 8,170 ML consumed on site, and no replenishment is claimed against it.
- Moving the target basis from consumption to withdrawal makes the denominator larger and is genuinely more demanding, but it also converts the pledge into one about water borrowed and returned rather than water depleted.
