# Efficiency Is Not Enough: Critical Perspective on Sustainable AI

**Topic:** Water Consumption - Critical AI Sustainability Framework
**Source:** Wright, Igel, Samuel, Selvan (2025)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3724500

## Insight

This 2025 Communications of the ACM paper argues that the AI sustainability discourse's focus on efficiency gains misses the fundamental problem: efficiency improvements tend to be overwhelmed by increases in overall AI usage (Jevons paradox). The authors argue that "efficiency is not enough" and that meaningful AI sustainability requires addressing total consumption, not just per-unit efficiency. This challenges the narrative that better cooling or more efficient chips will solve AI's environmental footprint.

## Key Arguments

| Argument | Implication for Water |
|----------|----------------------|
| Efficiency gains get consumed by demand growth | More efficient cooling doesn't help if AI usage scales faster |
| Focus on efficiency deflects from consumption limits | Water-positive pledges mean nothing if total water use grows |
| Systemic approach needed, not just technical fixes | Water policy for AI must address demand, not just supply-side efficiency |
| Current metrics (PUE, WUE) insufficient | New frameworks needed to capture total environmental burden |

## Critical Finding

The paper represents a direct challenge to the industry narrative that technological improvements (better chips, improved cooling, renewable energy) will solve AI's environmental problems. For water consumption specifically, this means:

- Water-efficient cooling systems don't help if data center construction outpaces efficiency gains
- Industry self-reported WUE improvements may mask total water consumption growth
- Policy focus should be on absolute limits, not just efficiency targets

## Relevance to Water Research

This paper provides theoretical grounding for skepticism about corporate "water positive" and efficiency claims. The Jevons paradox framework explains why Google/Microsoft/Meta water efficiency improvements coexist with rising absolute water consumption. Published in CACM alongside Li et al. "Making AI Less Thirsty" - signals academic establishment recognizing systemic sustainability concerns.
