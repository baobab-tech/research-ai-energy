# Low-Quality Offsets: Lessons for Water Positive Claims

**Topic:** Water Consumption - Claims Scrutiny
**Source:** Trencher, Nick, Carlson, Johnson (2024)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-51151-w

## Insight

This Nature Communications study reveals that major companies systematically purchase low-quality carbon offsets, undermining the integrity of voluntary climate commitments. While focused on carbon, the methodology and findings directly apply to "water positive" claims: both rely on offset/replenishment schemes where quality is poorly regulated and verification is weak. If major corporations purchase questionable carbon offsets despite public sustainability commitments, similar skepticism should apply to water offset claims.

## Key Claims vs Reality

- **Claim:** Corporations offset environmental impacts through verified programs
- **Reality:** Major companies purchase low-quality offsets that may not deliver claimed benefits; voluntary markets lack rigorous verification standards

## Parallel to Water Claims

- Water "replenishment" projects often occur in different watersheds than water withdrawal
- Like carbon offsets, water credits lack standardized quality assessment
- Corporate reporting on water restoration typically lacks third-party verification
- Temporal mismatches exist: water consumed now vs water "restored" years later

## Notes

- Nature Communications publication - high credibility
- 55 citations indicates strong academic engagement
- Methodology could be adapted to analyze corporate water offset quality
- Establishes precedent that corporate sustainability claims warrant scrutiny
