# Benefits and Limitations of Recycled Water Systems in the Building Sector

**Topic:** Water Consumption - Reuse/Recycling
**Source:** Chen, Chen, Liu, Lichtfouse, Jiang, Hua, Osman, Farghali, Huang, Zhang, Rooney & Yap (2024)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10311-023-01683-2

## Insight

This review examines recycled water systems for building construction and operation, including greywater recycling technologies applicable to industrial cooling. The paper finds that incorporating recycled water can enhance consistency and workability of water use while identifying key challenges: water quality assurance, dual piping infrastructure, and regulatory compliance. For data centers, which are effectively large industrial buildings with significant water demands, these findings inform the feasibility of on-site water recycling.

## Key Data

- Recycled water enhances consistency and workability of reclaimed water by 12-14%
- Concrete viscosity increases by 11% with recycled water
- Yield stress increases by 25% with recycled water
- Technologies: membrane filtration and advanced oxidation processes

## Reuse Technologies

- Greywater recycling systems for building integration
- Membrane filtration (ultrafiltration, nanofiltration)
- Advanced oxidation processes for water treatment
- Dual piping infrastructure required for separate systems

## Challenges Identified

- Water quality assurance requirements
- Need for dual piping infrastructure (additional capital cost)
- Regulatory compliance across jurisdictions
- Coordination between policymakers, developers, and practitioners

## Policy Drivers

- Government incentives accelerate adoption
- Mandates and subsidy policies drive implementation
- Standardization of recycled water quality needed
- Building codes may require water recycling in future
