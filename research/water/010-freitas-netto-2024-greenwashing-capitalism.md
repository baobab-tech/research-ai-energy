# Greenwashing Theory: Why Corporate Environmental Claims Require Skepticism

**Topic:** Water Consumption - Claims Scrutiny
**Source:** de Freitas Netto, Sobral, Ribeiro, Soares (2024)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/gec3.12736

## Insight

This Geography Compass paper develops a critical theory of greenwashing, arguing it is not merely "bad behavior" by individual corporations but a systemic feature of contemporary capitalism. The authors contend that greenwashing obscures social and ecological relations, enabling business-as-usual while projecting environmental commitment. This framework applies directly to "water positive" claims: the terminology itself may function as greenwashing by framing continued water consumption growth as environmentally responsible through offset mechanisms.

## Key Claims vs Reality

- **Claim:** Corporate environmental commitments represent genuine sustainability efforts
- **Reality:** Greenwashing processes obscure actual environmental impacts; "green" capitalism may be structurally incompatible with sustainability; appearance of environmental action substitutes for substantive change

## Theoretical Implications for Water Claims

- "Water positive" language may normalize continued water extraction growth
- Focus on future pledges (2030 targets) deflects from present consumption
- Replenishment narratives shift attention from withdrawal impacts
- Corporate disclosure frameworks may systematically favor appearance over substance
