# Single-Phase Immersion Cooling for High-Energy Density Server Racks

**Topic:** Water Consumption - Alternative Cooling Technology
**Source:** Kim, Choi, Lee, Lee (2025)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2025.125476

## Insight

This 2025 Applied Thermal Engineering paper presents computational analysis of single-phase immersion cooling for high-density AI server racks. Immersion cooling submerges servers in dielectric fluid rather than using water-based evaporative cooling, potentially eliminating data center water consumption while handling the extreme heat from AI accelerators. This represents a technical pathway to "waterless" data center cooling.

## Key Technical Findings

| Parameter | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| Target application | High-energy density server racks (AI/HPC) |
| Cooling method | Single-phase immersion in dielectric fluid |
| Water requirement | Zero direct water consumption |
| Heat dissipation | Capable of handling extreme GPU thermal loads |
| Trade-off | Higher capital cost, specialized infrastructure |

## Water Consumption Implications

**Potential benefits:**
- Eliminates evaporative cooling water consumption
- No cooling tower blowdown (major water loss source)
- No water treatment chemicals needed
- Closed-loop system with no water loss

**Caveats:**
- Dielectric fluids have environmental concerns (manufacturing, disposal)
- Not yet widely deployed at hyperscale
- Higher upfront infrastructure costs
- Embodied water in fluid production not analyzed

## Relevance to AI Water Research

Immersion cooling offers a technical path to near-zero operational water consumption for AI data centers. However:
- Current deployment is limited (most data centers still use evaporative cooling)
- Corporate water reduction claims via immersion cooling need verification
- Lifecycle water impacts (fluid manufacturing) not well-studied
- Cost barrier limits adoption, especially in existing facilities
