# Earth System Boundaries: The Gap Between Corporate Claims and Planetary Limits

**Topic:** Water Consumption - Claims Scrutiny
**Source:** Bai, Hasan, Andersen, Bjorn, Kilkis, Ospina, Liu, Cornell et al. (2024)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41893-023-01255-w

## Insight

This Nature Sustainability paper develops a framework for translating planetary boundaries into actionable targets for cities and businesses. Critically, it exposes the gap between corporate sustainability claims and actual safe operating limits. The authors argue that current corporate water accounting often fails to account for local watershed stress, cumulative impacts, and absolute planetary boundaries. A company can claim to be "water positive" while still contributing to regional water scarcity if its consumption occurs in stressed watersheds.

## Key Claims vs Reality

- **Claim:** Companies achieving "water positive" status operate sustainably
- **Reality:** Corporate water accounting typically uses global aggregates rather than watershed-specific impact assessments; replenishment in non-stressed areas does not offset extraction from water-scarce regions

## Key Framework Insights

- Water consumption must be assessed against local watershed capacity, not global averages
- Corporate boundaries (operational, value chain) often exclude significant impacts
- Temporal and spatial mismatches in offset claims undermine environmental benefit
- "Net positive" framing can obscure absolute consumption growth
