# Water as Resistance: Local Communities Fighting AI Infrastructure

**Topic:** Water Consumption - Direct Use
**Source:** Lehuede, S. (2024)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-024-01922-2

## Insight

This paper documents grassroots resistance to AI water consumption in Chile, where communities are actively fighting Google's water-intensive data center project in Santiago and lithium extraction (for AI hardware batteries) in the Atacama Desert affecting Lickan Antay Indigenous territory. The author develops an "elemental ethics" framework that centers local and Indigenous knowledge in AI ethics debates, arguing that standard top-down, utilitarian approaches to sustainable AI fail to capture the lived experience of communities bearing the environmental burden of AI infrastructure.

## Key Data

- Case study 1: Santiago community resistance to Google data center project
- Case study 2: Atacama Desert lithium extraction impacts on Lickan Antay Indigenous communities
- Framework: "Elemental ethics" - connecting AI's value chain to water, minerals, and ancestral approaches to nature

## Notes

- Perspective: Environmental humanities/ethics approach rather than quantitative measurement
- Key contribution: Shifts conversation from aggregate statistics to localized impacts and affected communities
- Highlights the colonial dimensions of AI infrastructure - water-stressed regions often bear the costs
- Academic research from an ethics perspective
- Complements quantitative water footprint studies by showing the human dimension
