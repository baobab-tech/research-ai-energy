# Google's Cerrillos Data Centre Was Permitted for 169 Litres per Second in a Drought-Stricken Santiago District, and the Figure Mobilised the Opposition That Stalled It

**Topic:** Water — siting conflict and local opposition to data centre water withdrawal; community-level evidence on how withdrawal figures enter public dispute
**Source:** Sebastián Lehuedé (King's College London; Centre of Governance and Human Rights, University of Cambridge), 2024
**Type:** peer-reviewed (*AI & Society*, open access CC-BY)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-024-01922-2
**Published:** 2024-04

## Finding

A single number from Google's environmental submission, 169 litres per second, became the organising fact of a neighbourhood campaign that stopped a data centre. Residents of Cerrillos, a working-class district of Santiago, formed MOSACAT in 2019 after reading the project's environmental report and extracting that withdrawal figure. They put it on bus-stop posters, forced a question onto a municipal referendum (49% rejecting the project against 38% approving), and by 2022 had extracted a commitment from Google not to use water for server cooling. As of December 2023 construction had not begun. The parallel case, lithium extraction from the Atacama Salt Flat, runs on a community-derived figure of 4,100 litres per second and a 2019 Chilean environmental court finding of scientific uncertainty about the aquifer.

The study's argument is that neither community can establish whether the withdrawal is sustainable, because no hydrogeological study of local groundwater availability exists in either area and none of the operator, the municipality or the municipal water utility has commissioned one. The asymmetry is between a precise, disclosed withdrawal rate and an undetermined denominator.

## Methodology

Qualitative fieldwork, March to April 2022. One in-depth interview with three MOSACAT members at their Cerrillos headquarters; a co-organised public workshop on 2 April 2022; participation in the World Water Day protest in Santiago on 2 March 2022; collection of MOSACAT protest material from their Facebook page. Three semi-structured interviews with the Council of Atacameño People, covering a former leader, a current member of the research group and a teacher critical of the Council. Analysis of a 2019 video of the Council's COP25 participation. Thematic analysis identifying how water was articulated, followed by material-semiotic analysis of interview quotes and protest graphics.

No measurement of water use was undertaken. Every water quantity in the paper comes from a project submission, a court document, a secondary source or a community's own figure.

## Limitations and conflicts

The author declares no conflict of interest. The work was supported by the Centre of Governance and Human Rights at the University of Cambridge. The author states a positionality of insider-outsider: raised and educated in Santiago, academic career in the UK, in contact with Lickan Antay community members since 2019 through earlier research on astronomy data governance, and identified by some community members as non-Indigenous Chilean. The study deliberately foregrounds activist perspectives and states that those views may not be shared by the wider communities. Interviewees consented to their real names but were anonymised in publication.

One quantitative comparison in the paper does not hold. The text states, citing Arellano et al. 2020 p. 201, that the project's 169 L/s "in 24 h equals the water consumed by the more than 80,000 Cerrillos residents in a year". At 169 L/s, a 24-hour period yields about 14,600 cubic metres, roughly 182 litres per resident, which is about one day of typical household use for that population, not one year. The comparison holds on an annual basis: 169 L/s sustained for a year is about 5.3 million cubic metres, or roughly 67 cubic metres per resident per year, which is consistent with typical Chilean municipal per-capita consumption. The "in 24 h" framing overstates by a factor of roughly 365.

The two Atacama figures in the paper, 4,100 L/s in the narrative and "almost two thousand litres per second" in the interview quote, are not reconciled, and neither is attributed to a measurement or a permit.

169 L/s is a permitted or planned withdrawal, not consumption, and not a figure for an operating facility. No WUE, no consumption-versus-withdrawal split, and no cooling design are available for the project. The Atacama case concerns lithium brine extraction for rechargeable batteries; the paper links it to AI through the device and value-chain framing rather than through AI hardware specifically.

The source contributes no measurement of data-centre water consumption. Its contribution is documentary: what was disclosed, what was contested, what was decided, and what remains unknown at one specific site.

## Relation to existing corpus

The only source in the corpus documenting a data-centre siting conflict from the affected community's side with named dates, figures and outcomes. It grounds the regional-disparity result in `research/water/019-li-2023-environmental-equity-regional-water.md`, which establishes dispersion of burden across sites in simulation but says nothing about how the burdened locality responds.

The missing-denominator problem it describes, a disclosed withdrawal rate against an unquantified local supply, is the same gap that `research/water/034-crs-2026-data-centers-water-faq.md` and `research/water/035-gaster-itif-2026-cooling-technology-water-energy-tradeoff.md` identify at national level, where both note that watershed conditions rather than national totals determine impact.

The documented shift from a water-cooled to a no-water design under public pressure is a data point on the corporate-disclosure question raised by `research/water/031-google-2026-fy2025-water-disclosure.md`: the commitment here is asserted, never accompanied by a published design or a site figure.
